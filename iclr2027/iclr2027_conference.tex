\begin{abstract}
Fair representation learning seeks a representation that is informative for downstream prediction and independent of a sensitive attribute.  For a continuous sensitive attribute, most existing methods compare a conditional quantity with its marginal counterpart at each value of the attribute and average the resulting discrepancy over those values.  Such an average can remain small when a large discrepancy is concentrated in a region of the sensitive domain that has low probability.  We introduce $L_{\infty}$-generalized demographic parity for predictions, which replaces this average by a supremum.  Its representation-level counterpart is the supremum integral probability metric (supIPM), the largest discrepancy over the sensitive domain between the conditional and marginal distributions of the representation.  Our estimator of supIPM requires no binning.  The conditional distribution at each sensitive value is estimated by an empirical distribution with kernel weights normalized to sum to one, and its discrepancy from the marginal distribution is measured by ReLU-IPM, the integral probability metric whose discriminators are single rectified linear units (ReLU) with unit-norm weight vector and bounded bias.  These two choices yield a finite-dimensional adversarial objective that can be optimized with minibatches.  A comparison inequality between ReLU-IPM and the integral probability metric over a H\"older ball extends the fairness guarantee for ReLU discriminators to prediction heads of bounded H\"older norm.  Under smoothness and positive-density conditions, the empirical supIPM converges to the population supIPM uniformly over a class of ReLU network encoders that grows with the sample size, and the fairness level attained on the sample transfers to a population bound that holds uniformly over the sensitive domain and over prediction heads of bounded H\"older norm.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A learned representation serves many downstream tasks, including tasks that are unspecified when the representation is trained \citep{bengio2013representation}.  This transferability makes the fairness requirement more demanding, because constraining one prediction head does not prevent another from exploiting sensitive information retained in the features \citep{madras2018adversarially}.  Fair representation learning (FRL) addresses this problem by seeking a representation that is informative for prediction and independent of the sensitive attribute $S$, so that every measurable function of the representation is again independent of $S$ \citep{zemel2013learning}.

Most FRL methods assume that $S$ is categorical, in which case demographic parity (DP) requires the distribution of the prediction to be the same across the observed groups, and these methods enforce the requirement by matching the group-conditional distributions of the representation.  One line of work trains an adversary to recover group membership from the representation \citep{edwards2016censoring,madras2018adversarially}.  Another compares the group-conditional distributions through the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) of a fixed kernel \citep{louizos2016variational}, an integral probability metric (IPM) over a prespecified parametric family of discriminators \citep{kim2022parametric}, or the power of an MMD two-sample test with a learned kernel \citep{deka2023mmdbfair}.  This construction does not extend directly to a continuous $S$ such as age or income.  Distinct observations take distinct sensitive values with probability one, so at most one observation is available at any given $s$, and one observation does not suffice to estimate the conditional distribution at $S=s$.  Binning creates such groups artificially, but the resulting criterion depends on the choice of cutpoints \citep{kong2025fair}, and the discretization introduces threshold effects and discards the ordering of the sensitive values \citep{mary2019continuous}.

Existing methods for a continuous $S$ have largely focused on the fitted predictor, measuring its dependence on $S$ through maximal correlation or through generalized demographic parity (GDP), the average over $S$ of the absolute difference between the conditional and marginal mean predictions \citep{mary2019continuous,grari2020continuous,jiang2022gdp}.  \citet{kong2025fair} take the same average to the representation level: the expectation of integral probability metrics (EIPM) is the average over $S$ of the IPM between the conditional distribution of the representation given $S=s$ and its marginal distribution.  Their algorithm FREM penalizes an estimate of EIPM in which the conditional distributions are estimated by normalized kernel weights and the inner IPM is the MMD \citep{gretton2012kernel}.  A large discrepancy confined to a narrow range of sensitive values contributes little to this average when that range has low probability, whereas categorical DP applies the same tolerance to every group regardless of its probability.

Two recent criteria are more closely related to ours.  \citet{kim2024probable} replace the average by a maximum over segments of the sensitive domain that have probability at least $\alpha$, averaging the prediction within each segment.  \citet{ni2026joint} compare the joint distribution of the representation and $S$ with the product of their marginal distributions, and their bound controls a kernel-smoothed version of the discrepancy between the conditional and marginal distributions at each $s$ rather than that discrepancy itself.  Neither criterion, therefore, constrains the discrepancy between the conditional and marginal distributions of the representation at every $s$.

In this paper we replace the average over $S$ by a supremum, so that the same tolerance applies at every $s$.  GDP then becomes $L_{\infty}$-generalized demographic parity ($L_{\infty}$-GDP) for predictions, and EIPM becomes the supremum integral probability metric (supIPM) for representations.  Our estimator of supIPM requires no binning: it estimates the conditional distribution at an arbitrary $s$ with kernel weights normalized to sum to one and maximizes its discrepancy from the marginal distribution over the sensitive domain.  The inner discrepancy is the ReLU-IPM of \citet{park2025relu}, whose discriminators are single rectified linear units (ReLU) with unit-norm weight vector and bounded bias, so its defining supremum is a finite-dimensional problem that can be solved with minibatches.  We map the representation into the unit ball, where ReLU-IPM separates probability distributions.  A comparison inequality between ReLU-IPM and the IPM over a H\"older ball then extends a supIPM bound, uniformly over the sensitive domain, to prediction heads of bounded H\"older norm.  We also prove that the empirical supIPM converges to its population counterpart uniformly over a class of ReLU network encoders that grows with the sample size.  Because the convergence is uniform over the encoder class, the result applies to the data-dependent encoder returned by training.  The population supIPM of that encoder exceeds its empirical supIPM by at most the uniform deviation between the two over the encoder class, so the fairness level attained on the sample transfers to the population.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We introduce $L_{\infty}$-GDP for predictions and supIPM for representations.  Both extend the groupwise requirement of categorical DP to a continuous sensitive attribute by measuring the largest discrepancy over the sensitive domain.
  \item We estimate supIPM without binning, from normalized kernel weights and ReLU-IPM, and obtain a finite-dimensional adversarial procedure that is compatible with minibatch training.
  \item We derive fairness bounds that hold uniformly over the sensitive domain for prediction heads of bounded H\"older norm, and we prove uniform convergence of the empirical supIPM over a class of ReLU network encoders that grows with the sample size.
\end{itemize}

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

\subsection{Fair representation using EIPM}
\label{sec:frem}

We observe an input $\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{X}$, an outcome $Y\in\mathcal{Y}$, and a continuous sensitive attribute $S$ taking values in the sensitive domain $\mathcal{S}$, a compact interval of $\mathbb{R}$.  An encoder $h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}$ maps $\mathbf{X}$ to a representation $\mathbf{Z}=h(\mathbf{X})$ in the representation space $\mathcal{Z}=\mathbb{R}^d$, where $d$ is the representation dimension.  A prediction head $f:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}$ maps the representation to a real-valued prediction, and $g=f\circ h$ denotes the composed prediction model.  When the encoder and the prediction head are parameterized, we write them as $h_{\phi}$ and $f_{\psi}$ with parameters $\phi$ and $\psi$.  We write $\mathbb{P}_S$ for the distribution of $S$, $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ for the marginal distribution of $\mathbf{Z}$, and $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}$ for the conditional distribution of $\mathbf{Z}$ given $S=s$.  Equality $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ for $\mathbb{P}_S$-almost every $s$ is equivalent to independence of $\mathbf{Z}$ and $S$, and this independence is inherited by every measurable prediction based on $\mathbf{Z}$.  FRL seeks an encoder for which $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}$ is close to $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ over $\mathcal{S}$.

Let $\mathcal{V}$ be a class of real-valued discriminators on $\mathcal{Z}$.  The IPM associated with $\mathcal{V}$ between two probability measures $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})
  :=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
  \left|\int v(\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{z})-\int v(\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{z})\right|.
  \label{eq:prelim-ipm}
\end{equation}
We say that $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}$ separates probability distributions if $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})=0$ implies $\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{Q}$.  The class $\mathcal{V}$ determines which differences between $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ the IPM detects.  It also determines which prediction heads the IPM controls, because $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})$ bounds $\left|\int v\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}-\int v\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}\right|$ for every $v\in\mathcal{V}$, and a prediction head that lies in $\mathcal{V}$ is one such $v$.

For a continuous sensitive attribute, \citet{kong2025fair} average the IPM between the conditional and marginal distributions over the distribution of $S$ and define EIPM as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)
  :=\int_{\mathcal{S}}
    \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s},\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}})
  \,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_S(s).
  \label{eq:eipm}
\end{equation}
If $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}$ separates probability distributions, then $\operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)=0$ implies $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ for $\mathbb{P}_S$-almost every $s$.  The quantity $\operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)$ also bounds the generalized demographic parity of every prediction head in $\mathcal{V}$, a criterion defined in Section~\ref{sec:supipm-criterion}.  For a head that admits a uniform approximation by functions in $\mathcal{V}$, the same bound holds up to the error of its best uniform approximation \citep{kong2025fair}.

For a categorical sensitive attribute, each conditional distribution can be estimated by the empirical distribution of the observations in the corresponding group.  When $S$ is continuous, with probability one no two observations share a sensitive value, so this groupwise construction is not consistent.  FREM instead estimates the conditional distribution at each observed $S_i$ by kernel smoothing.  Given the sample $\mathcal{D}=\{(\mathbf{X}_i,S_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ and $\mathbf{Z}_i=h(\mathbf{X}_i)$, FREM defines
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i),\gamma}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=S_i}
  :=\sum_{j\ne i}\widehat{w}^{(-i)}_{\gamma}(j)\,\delta_{\mathbf{Z}_j},
  \qquad
  \widehat{w}^{(-i)}_{\gamma}(j)
  :=\frac{K_{\gamma}(S_j,S_i)}{\sum_{r\ne i}K_{\gamma}(S_r,S_i)},
  \label{eq:frem-conditional}
\end{equation}
where $K_{\gamma}$ is a kernel on $\mathcal{S}$ with bandwidth $\gamma>0$, and the superscript $(-i)$ indicates that observation $i$ is left out and the weights are centered at $S_i$.  The weights $\widehat{w}^{(-i)}_{\gamma}(j)$ sum to one over $j\ne i$, and they place more mass on observations whose sensitive values lie closer to $S_i$.  FREM compares this kernel-weighted conditional distribution with the leave-one-out empirical marginal distribution $\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i)}_{\mathbf{Z}}=(n-1)^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{\mathbf{Z}_j}$, which yields the estimator
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\operatorname{EIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)
  :=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}
  \left(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i),\gamma}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=S_i},
        \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i)}_{\mathbf{Z}}\right).
  \label{eq:eipm-estimator}
\end{equation}

With a general discriminator class, this estimator would require a separate maximization at every $S_i$.  FREM removes these maximizations by taking $\mathcal{V}$ to be the unit ball of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS).  Each IPM is then the MMD, which has a closed-form kernel expression \citep{gretton2012kernel,kong2025fair}.  For a per-sample loss $\ell$, FREM trains the encoder and the prediction head by minimizing the penalized empirical risk
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \ell\!\left(f(\mathbf{Z}_i),Y_i\right)
  +\lambda\,\widehat{\operatorname{EIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S),
  \label{eq:frem-objective}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda\ge 0$ controls the accuracy--fairness trade-off.

\subsection{ReLU-IPM}
\label{sec:relu-ipm}

The closed-form expression of the MMD makes the EIPM estimator computationally feasible.  With $\mathcal{V}$ the unit ball of an RKHS, however, the class of prediction heads for which EIPM provides a guarantee is determined by that RKHS.  \citet{park2025relu} observe that the RKHS of the Gaussian kernel contains only infinitely differentiable functions.  For a prediction head of lower smoothness, the EIPM guarantee then holds only up to the error of its best uniform approximation by functions in the unit ball of that RKHS.  They address this restriction with ReLU-IPM, an IPM whose discriminator class is indexed by a finite-dimensional parameter.  ReLU-IPM nevertheless upper bounds the IPM over nonparametric H\"older balls.

Let $\mathbb{B}^d:=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^d:\lVert \mathbf{z}\rVert_2\le 1\}$ be the unit ball and $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^d:\lVert\theta\rVert_2=1\}$ the unit sphere.  \citet{park2025relu} define the ReLU discriminator class on $\mathbb{B}^d$ by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}
  :=\left\{
    v_{\theta,\mu}(\mathbf{z})=(\theta^\top \mathbf{z}+\mu)_+:
    \theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\ \mu\in[-1,1]
  \right\},
  \label{eq:relu-class}
\end{equation}
where $(a)_+:=\max\{a,0\}$.  The ReLU-IPM between probability distributions $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$ is $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})$, that is, Equation~\ref{eq:prelim-ipm} with $\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$.  Although $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$ is indexed by the finite-dimensional parameter $(\theta,\mu)$ alone, ReLU-IPM separates probability distributions on $\mathbb{B}^d$ \citep{park2025relu}.

For a smoothness index $\beta>0$, let $k$ be the largest integer strictly smaller than $\beta$.  The $\beta$-H\"older norm of a $k$ times continuously differentiable function $v:\mathbb{B}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ is the largest of the following quantities: the supremum norms of the partial derivatives of $v$ of order at most $k$, and the H\"older seminorms of order $\beta-k$ of the partial derivatives of order $k$.  Its unit ball is the $\beta$-H\"older ball $\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$, and we write H\"older-IPM for $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}$.  \citet{park2025relu} establish that, for every $\beta>0$ with $\beta\ne(d+3)/2$, there exists a constant $C$ depending only on $d$ and $\beta$ such that, for all probability measures $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})
  \le C
  \begin{cases}
    \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})^{\frac{2\beta}{d+3}},
      & \beta<\frac{d+3}{2},\\[2mm]
    \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q}),
      & \beta>\frac{d+3}{2}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:relu-holder}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:relu-holder} shows that a small ReLU-IPM implies a small H\"older-IPM even though the ReLU discriminator class is parametric.  We use this bound to translate a supIPM constraint into fairness guarantees for prediction heads of bounded H\"older norm.

Let $\mathbf{Z}'_1,\ldots,\mathbf{Z}'_{n'}$ be a second sample of size $n'$.  For the empirical measures $\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_n=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{\mathbf{Z}_i}$ and $\widehat{\mathbb{Q}}_{n'}=(n')^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n'}\delta_{\mathbf{Z}'_j}$, ReLU-IPM takes the form
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\,\mu\in[-1,1]}
  \left|
    \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n(\theta^\top \mathbf{Z}_i+\mu)_+
    -\frac{1}{n'}\sum_{j=1}^{n'}(\theta^\top \mathbf{Z}'_j+\mu)_+
  \right|.
  \label{eq:empirical-relu-ipm}
\end{equation}
\citet{park2025relu} compute this supremum by gradient ascent over $(\theta,\mu)$, projecting $\theta$ back onto the unit sphere after each step.  Because the ascent may converge to a local maximum, they repeat it from several random initializations and retain the largest value.  Section~\ref{sec:supipm-objective} uses the same finite-dimensional maximization to evaluate the inner discrepancy in supIPM.

\section{supIPM for fair representation learning}
\label{sec:supipm}

\subsection{From GDP to supIPM}
\label{sec:supipm-criterion}

We begin with the prediction-level criterion that motivates supIPM.  \citet{jiang2022gdp} define GDP for a continuous sensitive attribute as the $\mathbb{P}_S$-average of the absolute difference between the conditional and the marginal mean prediction, and \citet{kong2025fair} state the same criterion with the prediction replaced by a measurable transformation $\varphi$ of it.  For the composed prediction model $g=f\circ h$ and a measurable $\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, GDP is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{GDP}_{\varphi}(g)
  :=\mathbb{E}_{S}\!\left[
    \left|
      \mathbb{E}[\varphi(g(\mathbf{X}))\mid S]
      -\mathbb{E}[\varphi(g(\mathbf{X}))]
    \right|
  \right].
  \label{eq:gdp}
\end{equation}
The choice of $\varphi$ determines which functional of the prediction is compared.  The identity function compares conditional and marginal mean predictions, whereas a threshold indicator compares positive prediction rates.  Following FREM, we take $\varphi$ to be the identity function and write $\operatorname{GDP}(g)$.

Because GDP integrates the discrepancy between the conditional and marginal mean predictions against $\mathbb{P}_S$, a large discrepancy confined to a region of small $\mathbb{P}_S$-probability can contribute little to $\operatorname{GDP}(g)$.  To require a uniform bound over all values of the sensitive attribute, we replace the outer expectation by a supremum and define $L_{\infty}$-GDP, written $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}$, as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}(g)
  :=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
  \left|
    \mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s]
    -\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})]
  \right|.
  \label{eq:linfty-gdp}
\end{equation}

We assume throughout the two conditions that \citet{kong2025fair} impose in their Assumptions 2 and 3.  Under the positive-density condition, $S$ has a density $p_S$ with respect to the Lebesgue measure $\mathrm{Leb}$ on $\mathcal{S}$, and $0<c_S\le p_S(s)\le C_S<\infty$ for every $s\in\mathcal{S}$.  Under the smoothness condition, $(\mathbf{X},S)$ has a joint density $p(\mathbf{x},s)$ with respect to $\nu\otimes\mathrm{Leb}$ for a $\sigma$-finite measure $\nu$ on $\mathcal{X}$, and $p(\mathbf{x},s)$ is twice differentiable in $s$ with a bounded second derivative for every $\mathbf{x}$.  The supremum in Equation~\ref{eq:linfty-gdp} requires the conditional expectation at every $s\in\mathcal{S}$ rather than for $\mathbb{P}_S$-almost every $s$, so we fix one such joint density $p$ and define the conditional expectation pointwise by $\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s]:=\int g(\mathbf{x})\,p(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x})/p_S(s)$.

\citet{kim2024probable} observe that a maximum disparity over a continuous $S$ is sensitive to outliers and is estimated with large variance where the density of $S$ is small.  They therefore average the prediction within each segment of $\mathcal{S}$ that has probability at least $\alpha$.  Their criterion, probable demographic parity, requires the mean prediction to be equal across all such segments, and its violation is measured by the largest difference between the segment means.  Because the prediction is still averaged within each segment, a discrepancy confined to a set of probability below $\alpha$ has little effect on the criterion.  We retain the supremum in the population criterion, and the kernel-smoothed conditional distributions constructed in Section~\ref{sec:supipm-estimation} control the variance of its estimator.

The value $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}(f\circ h)$ depends on the particular head $f$, so we next state a criterion for the representation itself, which controls an entire class of downstream heads at once.

Let $h_{\phi}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^d$ be an encoder parameterized by $\phi$ and write $\mathbf{Z}=h_{\phi}(\mathbf{X})$.  Because the ReLU discriminator class in Equation~\ref{eq:relu-class} is defined on $\mathbb{B}^d$, we map the representation into the unit ball by
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}
  :=\frac{\mathbf{Z}}{\sqrt{\lVert\mathbf{Z}\rVert_2^2+1}}
  \in\mathbb{B}^d.
  \label{eq:normalized-representation}
\end{equation}
From here on, prediction heads and discriminator classes are taken on $\mathbb{B}^d$ and applied to $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}$.  As with $\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s]$, we define $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ pointwise from the fixed joint density.  For a discriminator class $\mathcal{V}$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$, we define
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}
  \left(\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}\right) \notag\\
  &=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
  \left|
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\mid S=s\right]
    -\mathbb{E}\!\left[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\right]
  \right|.
  \label{eq:supipm}
\end{align}
The outer supremum runs over the sensitive values, and the inner supremum runs over the discriminator class.  For $\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$, the inner quantity is the ReLU-IPM of Section~\ref{sec:relu-ipm}.  Equation~\ref{eq:supipm} is the representation-level counterpart of $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}$, because it measures the largest discrepancy between the conditional and marginal distributions before a prediction head is chosen.

The supIPM criterion differs from $\operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$, that is, Equation~\ref{eq:eipm} with $\mathbf{Z}$ replaced by $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}$, only in that the expectation over $S$ is replaced by a supremum over $s\in\mathcal{S}$.  The two constraints nevertheless differ at any positive tolerance.  For a tolerance $\rho>0$, a supIPM bound of $\rho$ guarantees that, for every function in the discriminator class, the absolute difference between its conditional expectation given $S=s$ and its marginal expectation is at most $\rho$ at every $s$, whereas an EIPM bound of $\rho$ constrains only the average of that difference over $S$.  Since an average never exceeds a supremum, a supIPM bound implies an EIPM bound at the same level, whereas an EIPM bound does not imply a supIPM bound without further regularity in $s$.

\citet{ni2026joint} also work at the representation level, and they replace the family of conditional distributions by a single discrepancy that compares the joint distribution of the representation and $S$ with the product of their marginal distributions.  This substitution removes the localization bandwidth and admits a closed-form estimator that converges at the parametric rate, but their bound controls a kernel-smoothed version of the discrepancy between the conditional and marginal distributions at each $s$ rather than the discrepancy itself.

\subsection{Estimation of supIPM}
\label{sec:supipm-estimation}

For the sample $\mathcal{D}$ of Section~\ref{sec:frem}, let $\mathbf{Z}_i=h_{\phi}(\mathbf{X}_i)$ and $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i=\mathbf{Z}_i/\sqrt{\lVert\mathbf{Z}_i\rVert_2^2+1}$.  Estimating supIPM requires an estimate of the conditional distribution $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ at every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, whereas FREM requires it only at the observed values $S_i$.  We therefore adapt the kernel-weighted construction of Equation~\ref{eq:frem-conditional} to an arbitrary $s$.  Because $s$ is no longer tied to an observation, the leave-one-out correction is not needed, and all $n$ observations contribute to both the conditional and the marginal estimates:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)
  :=\frac{K_{\gamma}(S_i,s)}{\sum_{j=1}^n K_{\gamma}(S_j,s)},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}
  :=\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)\,
  \delta_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i}.
  \label{eq:supipm-conditional-estimator}
\end{equation}
Here $K_{\gamma}$ is a kernel on $\mathcal{S}$ with bandwidth $\gamma>0$.  Observations whose sensitive values are close to $s$ receive larger weights, and the weights sum to one over $i$, so that $\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ is a probability distribution.  The marginal distribution is estimated by $\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}:=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i}$.

Substituting the two empirical distributions into Equation~\ref{eq:supipm} yields
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  (\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  \left(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s},
        \widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}\right) \notag\\
  &=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
    \sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\,\mu\in[-1,1]}
  \left|
    \sum_{i=1}^n
    \left(\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)-\frac{1}{n}\right)
    (\theta^\top\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i+\mu)_+
  \right|.
  \label{eq:empirical-supipm}
\end{align}
The EIPM estimator in Equation~\ref{eq:eipm-estimator} evaluates the discrepancy at the observed sensitive values and averages it.  The estimator in Equation~\ref{eq:empirical-supipm} instead keeps the continuous scale of $S$ and takes the largest estimated discrepancy over $\mathcal{S}$.  When $K_{\gamma}$ is differentiable, the kernel weights are differentiable in $s$, so gradient ascent over $\mathcal{S}$ can search for the largest discrepancy, and Section~\ref{sec:supipm-objective} performs this search during training.

\subsection{Learning with the supIPM regularizer}
\label{sec:supipm-objective}

We use the empirical supIPM as a regularizer and train the encoder $h_{\phi}$ and the prediction head $f_{\psi}$ jointly by minimizing
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\phi,\psi)
  :=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
  \ell\!\left(f_{\psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i),Y_i\right)
  +\lambda\,
  \widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  (\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S),
  \label{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is the per-sample loss of Section~\ref{sec:frem} and $\lambda\ge0$ controls the accuracy--fairness trade-off.  Fix a fairness level $\rho\ge0$ and consider minimizing the empirical supervised risk $n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\ell(f_{\psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i),Y_i)$ subject to $\widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)\le\rho$.  As in FREM, Equation~\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective} is the Lagrangian relaxation of this constrained problem.

We approximate the outer supremum in Equation~\ref{eq:empirical-supipm} by a search over $T$ candidate sensitive values in $\mathcal{S}$, and the inner supremum by a search over $M$ ReLU discriminators with parameters $(\theta_m,\mu_m)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]$ for $m\in[M]:=\{1,\ldots,M\}$.  For each $m\in[M]$, define
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_m(s)
  :=\left|
    \sum_{i=1}^n
    \left(\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)-\frac{1}{n}\right)
    (\theta_m^\top\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i+\mu_m)_+
  \right|.
  \label{eq:discriminator-discrepancy}
\end{equation}
With the discriminators held fixed, we initialize $s^{(1)},\ldots,s^{(T)}$ at distinct points of $\mathcal{S}$ and update them by gradient ascent on $\sum_{t=1}^{T}\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta_m(s^{(t)})$, projecting each iterate back onto $\mathcal{S}$ after every step.  Multiple initializations reduce the risk that the search over $s$ terminates at a poor local maximum.  After the ascent steps, we select
\begin{equation}
  s^\star\in\argmax_{s\in\{s^{(1)},\ldots,s^{(T)}\}}
  \max_{m\in[M]} \Delta_m(s).
  \label{eq:sensitive-candidate}
\end{equation}
The selected $s^\star$ is then held fixed while the discriminator parameters are updated by gradient ascent on $\sum_{m=1}^M\Delta_m(s^\star)$, and after each update $\theta_m$ is projected back onto the unit sphere and $\mu_m$ onto $[-1,1]$.  We alternate the sensitive-value update and the discriminator update, performing a prescribed number of discriminator steps in all, and we then search once more over the sensitive values with the updated discriminators.

The resulting value $\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta_m(s^\star)$ approximates the empirical supIPM in Equation~\ref{eq:empirical-supipm} and serves as the penalty term in Equation~\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}.  With this penalty in place, $\phi$ and $\psi$ are updated by gradient descent on $\mathcal{L}(\phi,\psi)$.  Equations~\ref{eq:discriminator-discrepancy} and \ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective} are built from sample averages over the observations, so the procedure applies without modification when these averages are computed on the current minibatch.

\section{General formatting instructions}
\label{gen_inst}

The text must be confined within a rectangle 5.5~inches (33~picas) wide and
9~inches (54~picas) long. The left margin is 1.5~inch (9~picas).
Use 10~point type with a vertical spacing of 11~points. Times New Roman is the
preferred typeface throughout. Paragraphs are separated by 1/2~line space,
with no indentation.

Paper title is 17~point, in small caps and left-aligned.
All pages should start at 1~inch (6~picas) from the top of the page.

Authors' names are
set in boldface, and each name is placed above its corresponding
address. The lead author's name is to be listed first, and
the co-authors' names are set to follow. Authors sharing the
same address can be on the same line.

Please pay special attention to the instructions in section \ref{others}
regarding figures, tables, acknowledgments, and references.


There will be a strict upper limit of \textbf{9 pages} for the main text of the initial submission, with unlimited additional pages for citations. This limit will be expanded to \textbf{10 pages} for rebuttal/camera ready.
