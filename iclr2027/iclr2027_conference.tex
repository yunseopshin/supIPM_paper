\begin{abstract}
Fair representation learning (FRL) seeks a representation that is informative for downstream prediction yet independent of a sensitive attribute. For a continuous sensitive attribute, existing methods measure the discrepancy between conditional and marginal distributions at each sensitive value and \emph{average} it over the distribution of the sensitive attribute. Such an average can be small even when a large discrepancy is confined to a low-probability range of sensitive values, where the criterion then provides no guarantee. So, we propose $L_\infty$-generalized demographic parity, which replaces the average by a supremum, and its representation-level counterpart, the supremum integral probability metric (supIPM). We then propose supIPM-FRL, which learns a representation under a constraint on the empirical supIPM. The procedure estimates conditional distributions by kernel weighting and measures their discrepancy from the marginal distribution by ReLU-IPM. 
% Such a discriminator is determined by a direction and a bias alone, so each discrepancy is maximized over the discriminator class by gradient ascent. 
We prove that the empirical supIPM is a uniformly consistent estimator, so the fairness level attained on the sample transfers to the population. 
% The transfer is uniform over the sensitive domain and over prediction heads of bounded H\"older norm. 
Experiments on tabular and graph benchmarks show that supIPM-FRL attains prediction performance comparable to existing methods while substantially reducing the worst discrepancy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A learned representation serves many downstream tasks, including tasks that are unspecified when the representation is trained \citep{bengio2013representation}.  This transferability makes the fairness requirement harder to satisfy because constraining one prediction head does not prevent another from exploiting sensitive information retained in the representation \citep{madras2018adversarially}.  Fair representation learning (FRL) addresses this problem by seeking a representation that is informative for prediction and independent of the sensitive attribute $S$ \citep{zemel2013learning}.
%, so that every measurable function of the representation is again independent of $S$


Most existing FRL methods have been developed for a categorical $S$, where each value of $S$ defines a group.  In this case, demographic parity (DP) requires the prediction to have the same distribution in every group, and these methods enforce DP by aligning the distribution of the representation across groups.  One line of work trains an adversary to predict the group from the representation \citep{edwards2016censoring,madras2018adversarially}.  Another compares the distribution of the representation across groups through the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) of a fixed kernel \citep{louizos2016variational}, an integral probability metric (IPM) over a prespecified parametric discriminator class \citep{kim2022parametric}, or the power of an MMD two-sample test with a learned kernel \citep{deka2023mmdbfair}.  This groupwise construction does not extend directly to a continuous $S$ such as age or income.  Distinct observations take distinct sensitive values with probability one, so at most one observation is available at any given sensitive value $s$, and one observation does not suffice to estimate the conditional distribution of the representation at $S=s$.  Discretizing $S$ makes the groupwise construction applicable, but the resulting criterion depends on how the bins are chosen \citep{kong2025fair}. 
It can also split nearly identical values into different groups, and it ignores their ordering \citep{mary2019continuous}.
% The discretization also introduces threshold effects and discards the ordering of the sensitive values \citep{mary2019continuous}.

Existing methods that treat $S$ as continuous have largely focused on the fitted predictor rather than on the representation, measuring its dependence on $S$ through maximal correlation or through generalized demographic parity (GDP) \citep{mary2019continuous,grari2020continuous,jiang2022gdp}. In particular, GDP is the average over $S$ of the absolute difference between the conditional and marginal mean predictions.  \citet{kong2025fair} apply the same average at the representation level: the expectation of integral probability metrics (EIPM) is the average over $S$ of the IPM between the conditional distribution of the representation given $S=s$ and its marginal distribution.  Both criteria average the discrepancy over the distribution of $S$, so a large discrepancy confined to a low-probability range of sensitive values contributes little to the average.  The average thus departs from categorical DP, which imposes one fairness level on every group regardless of its probability.

A new criterion for a continuous $S$ should therefore hold at every sensitive value.  For a representation, the criterion should also hold for every prediction head that may later be fitted on it.  In this paper we propose criteria that take the supremum over $S$ instead of the average, so that a single bound holds uniformly over the sensitive domain.  This replacement transforms GDP into $L_{\infty}$-generalized demographic parity ($L_{\infty}$-GDP) and EIPM into the supremum integral probability metric (supIPM).  Our estimator of supIPM obtains the conditional distribution at an arbitrary $s$ from kernel weights, without discretization, and searches the sensitive domain for the largest discrepancy.  The discrepancy is measured by the ReLU-IPM of \citet{park2025relu}, whose discriminators are single rectified linear units (ReLU) and which controls every prediction head of bounded H\"older norm.  We add this supIPM estimate as a penalty to the supervised risk and train the encoder and the prediction head with minibatches.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We propose $L_{\infty}$-GDP, a fairness criterion for prediction models, and supIPM, its representation-level counterpart.  Both extend the groupwise condition of categorical DP to a continuous sensitive attribute by measuring the worst discrepancy over the sensitive domain.
  \item We estimate supIPM from kernel weights and ReLU-IPM, and propose supIPM-FRL, an adversarial procedure that is compatible with minibatch training.
  \item We prove that the empirical supIPM is a uniformly consistent estimator, so that the fairness level attained on the sample transfers to the population.
  % uniformly over the sensitive domain and over prediction heads of bounded H\"older norm.
\end{itemize}

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

\subsection{Fair representation using EIPM (FREM)}
\label{sec:frem}

We observe an input $\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{X}$, an outcome $Y\in\mathcal{Y}$, and a continuous sensitive attribute $S$ taking values in the sensitive domain $\mathcal{S}$, a compact interval of $\mathbb{R}$.  An encoder $h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}$ maps $\mathbf{X}$ to a representation $\mathbf{Z}=h(\mathbf{X})$ in the representation space $\mathcal{Z}=\mathbb{R}^d$, where $d$ is the representation dimension.  A prediction head $f:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}$ maps the representation to a real-valued prediction, and $f\circ h$ denotes the composed prediction model.  When the encoder and the prediction head are parameterized, we write them as $h_{\phi}$ and $f_{\psi}$ with parameters $\phi$ and $\psi$.  We write $\mathbb{P}_S$ for the distribution of $S$, $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ for the marginal distribution of $\mathbf{Z}$, and $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}$ for the conditional distribution of $\mathbf{Z}$ given $S=s$.  
% Equality $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ for $\mathbb{P}_S$-almost every $s$ is equivalent to independence of $\mathbf{Z}$ and $S$, and this independence is inherited by every measurable function of $\mathbf{Z}$.

The discrepancy between $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}$ and $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ is measured through a discriminator class $\mathcal{V}$, a class of real-valued discriminator functions on $\mathcal{Z}$. The IPM associated with $\mathcal{V}$ between two probability measures $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})
  :=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
  \left|\int v(\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{z})-\int v(\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{z})\right|.
  \label{eq:prelim-ipm}
\end{equation}
We say that $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}$ separates probability distributions if $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})=0$ implies $\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{Q}$.  The class $\mathcal{V}$ determines which prediction heads the IPM controls, because $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})$ bounds $\left|\int v\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}-\int v\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}\right|$ for every $v\in\mathcal{V}$.

For a continuous sensitive attribute, \citet{kong2025fair} propose fair representation using EIPM (FREM).  They average, over the distribution of $S$, the IPM between the conditional and the marginal distribution, and define EIPM as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)
  :=\int_{\mathcal{S}}
    \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s},\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}})
  \,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_S(s).
  \label{eq:eipm}
\end{equation}
If $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}$ separates probability distributions, then $\operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)=0$ implies $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ for $\mathbb{P}_S$-almost every $s$.

FREM estimates the conditional distribution at each observed $S_i$ by kernel weighting.  Given the sample $\mathcal{D}=\{(\mathbf{X}_i,S_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ and $\mathbf{Z}_i=h(\mathbf{X}_i)$, FREM defines
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i),\gamma}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=S_i}
  :=\sum_{j\ne i}\widehat{w}^{(-i)}_{\gamma}(j)\,\delta_{\mathbf{Z}_j},
  \qquad
  \widehat{w}^{(-i)}_{\gamma}(j)
  :=\frac{K_{\gamma}(S_j,S_i)}{\sum_{r\ne i}K_{\gamma}(S_r,S_i)},
  \label{eq:frem-conditional}
\end{equation}
where $K_{\gamma}$ is a kernel on $\mathcal{S}$ with bandwidth $\gamma>0$, $\delta_{\mathbf{z}}$ denotes the point mass at $\mathbf{z}$, and the superscript $(-i)$ indicates that observation $i$ is left out.  FREM compares this kernel-weighted conditional distribution with the leave-one-out empirical marginal distribution $\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i)}_{\mathbf{Z}}=(n-1)^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{\mathbf{Z}_j}$, which yields the estimator
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\operatorname{EIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)
  :=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}
  \left(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i),\gamma}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=S_i},
        \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i)}_{\mathbf{Z}}\right).
  \label{eq:eipm-estimator}
\end{equation}
With a general discriminator class, this estimator requires a separate maximization at every $S_i$.  FREM avoids these maximizations by taking $\mathcal{V}$ to be the unit ball of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS), for which each IPM is the MMD and has a closed-form kernel expression \citep{gretton2012kernel,kong2025fair}.  For a supervised loss $\ell$ and a penalty parameter $\lambda\ge 0$, FREM then trains the encoder and the prediction head by minimizing
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \ell\!\left(f(\mathbf{Z}_i),Y_i\right)
  +\lambda\,\widehat{\operatorname{EIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S).
  \label{eq:frem-objective}
\end{equation}

\subsection{ReLU-IPM}
\label{sec:relu-ipm}

The RKHS unit ball of Section~\ref{sec:frem} determines the prediction heads that EIPM controls.  The RKHS of the Gaussian kernel, however, contains only infinitely differentiable functions \citep{park2025relu}, so a prediction head of lower smoothness lies outside $\mathcal{V}$.  \citet{park2025relu} address this restriction with ReLU-IPM, an IPM whose discriminator class is indexed by a finite-dimensional parameter and which nevertheless controls every prediction head of bounded H\"older norm.

Let $\mathbb{B}^d:=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^d:\lVert \mathbf{z}\rVert_2\le 1\}$ be the unit ball and $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^d:\lVert\theta\rVert_2=1\}$ the unit sphere.  \citet{park2025relu} define the ReLU discriminator class on $\mathbb{B}^d$ by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}
  :=\left\{
    v_{\theta,\mu}(\mathbf{z})=(\theta^\top \mathbf{z}+\mu)_+:
    \theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\ \mu\in[-1,1]
  \right\},
  \label{eq:relu-class}
\end{equation}
where $(a)_+:=\max\{a,0\}$.  The ReLU-IPM between probability distributions $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$ is $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})$, that is, Equation~(\ref{eq:prelim-ipm}) with $\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$.  Although $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$ is indexed by the finite-dimensional parameter $(\theta,\mu)$ alone, ReLU-IPM separates probability distributions on $\mathbb{B}^d$ \citep{park2025relu}.  The finite dimension of $(\theta,\mu)$ makes this supremum computable. \citet{park2025relu} evaluate ReLU-IPM between empirical distributions by gradient ascent over $(\theta,\mu)$, projecting $\theta$ back onto the unit sphere after each step.

To describe the prediction heads that ReLU-IPM controls, fix a smoothness index $\beta>0$ and let $k$ be the largest integer strictly smaller than $\beta$.  Let $\partial^{\mathbf{a}}$ denote the partial derivative indexed by a multi-index $\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}_0^d$ of order $|\mathbf{a}|=a_1+\cdots+a_d$.  Then the $\beta$-H\"older norm of a $k$ times continuously differentiable function $v:\mathbb{B}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ and the $\beta$-H\"older ball on $\mathbb{B}^d$ are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}}
  &:=\max\left\{
    \max_{|\mathbf{a}|\le k}\,\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{B}^d}
      \left|\partial^{\mathbf{a}}v(\mathbf{z})\right|,\;
    \max_{|\mathbf{a}|=k}\,\sup_{\mathbf{z}\ne\mathbf{z}'\in\mathbb{B}^d}
      \frac{\left|\partial^{\mathbf{a}}v(\mathbf{z})-\partial^{\mathbf{a}}v(\mathbf{z}')\right|}
           {\lVert\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}'\rVert_2^{\beta-k}}
  \right\},
  \\
  \mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d
  &:=\left\{v\in C^{k}(\mathbb{B}^d):\lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}}\le 1\right\},
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:holder-ball}
\end{equation}
and H\"older-IPM denotes $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}$.  \citet{park2025relu} establish that, for $d\ge3$ and every $\beta>0$ with $\beta\ne(d+3)/2$, there exists a constant $C$ depending only on $d$ and $\beta$ such that, for all probability measures $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})
  \le C
  \begin{cases}
    \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})^{\frac{2\beta}{d+3}},
      & \beta<\frac{d+3}{2},\\[2mm]
    \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q}),
      & \beta>\frac{d+3}{2}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:relu-holder}
\end{equation}
A bound on ReLU-IPM therefore implies a bound on the H\"older-IPM.

\section{supIPM for fair representation learning}
\label{sec:supipm}

\subsection{Uniform Fairness over the Sensitive Domain}
\label{sec:supipm-criterion}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{motivation.pdf}
  \caption{The conditional and the marginal distribution of the representation coincide at a typical sensitive value and differ at $s_0$, a sensitive value where few observations lie.}
  \label{fig:motivation}
\end{figure}

\citet{jiang2022gdp} define GDP for a continuous sensitive attribute as the average over $S$ of the absolute difference between the conditional and the marginal mean prediction.  For a bounded measurable function $g:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}$,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{GDP}(g)
  :=\mathbb{E}_{S}\!\left[
    \left|
      \mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S]
      -\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})]
    \right|
  \right].
  \label{eq:gdp}
\end{equation}

Because Equation~(\ref{eq:gdp}) averages this discrepancy over $S$, a large discrepancy confined to a region of small $\mathbb{P}_S$-probability can contribute little to $\operatorname{GDP}(g)$.  Figure~\ref{fig:motivation} illustrates such a case: the discrepancy between the conditional and the marginal distribution is large at the worst value $s_0$, whose neighborhood contributes too little probability to affect the average over $S$.  Categorical DP, by contrast, imposes one fairness level on every group regardless of its probability.  An extension to a continuous sensitive attribute should meet the same standard at every sensitive value.  We therefore replace the average by a supremum and define $L_{\infty}$-GDP, written $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}$, as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}(g)
  :=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
  \left|
    \mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s]
    -\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})]
  \right|.
  \label{eq:linfty-gdp}
\end{equation}
The supremum in Equation~(\ref{eq:linfty-gdp}) requires the conditional expectation at every $s\in\mathcal{S}$.
% , not only for $\mathbb{P}_S$-almost every $s$.  
We impose the following condition on the distribution of $S$.
\begin{assumption}[Positive density]
\label{as:positive-density}
$S$ has a density $p_S$ with respect to the Lebesgue measure $\mathrm{Leb}$ on $\mathcal{S}$, and $0<c_S\le p_S(s)\le C_S<\infty$ for every $s\in\mathcal{S}$.
\end{assumption}
Following \citet{kong2025fair}, we assume Assumption~\ref{as:positive-density} throughout.  Let $p(\mathbf{x},s)$ be a joint density of $(\mathbf{X},S)$ with respect to $\nu\otimes\mathrm{Leb}$ for a $\sigma$-finite measure $\nu$ on $\mathcal{X}$.  Fixing a version of $p$ with $\int_{\mathcal{X}} p(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x})=p_S(s)$ for every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, we obtain a version of the conditional expectation that is defined at every $s$, namely
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s]
  :=\frac{1}{p_S(s)}\int_{\mathcal{X}} g(\mathbf{x})\,p(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x}),
  \qquad s\in\mathcal{S},
  \label{eq:pointwise-conditional}
\end{equation}
and this version is used in what follows, in particular inside the supremum in Equation~(\ref{eq:linfty-gdp}).

The value $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}(f\circ h)$ can be evaluated only once the prediction head $f$ is fixed.  We therefore propose a criterion for the representation itself, which controls an entire class of prediction heads at once.  Such a criterion is  a discriminator class of the IPM .

Because the discriminator classes of Section~\ref{sec:relu-ipm} are defined on $\mathbb{B}^d$, we map the representation $\mathbf{Z}=h_{\phi}(\mathbf{X})$ of the parameterized encoder $h_{\phi}$ in Section~\ref{sec:frem} into the unit ball by $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}:=\mathbf{Z}/\sqrt{\lVert\mathbf{Z}\rVert_2^2+1}\in\mathbb{B}^d$.  From here on, prediction heads and discriminator classes are defined on $\mathbb{B}^d$ and applied to $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}$.  The conditional distribution $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ is defined as in Equation~(\ref{eq:pointwise-conditional}).  For a discriminator class $\mathcal{V}$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$, we define
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}
  \left(\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}\right) \notag\\
  &=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
  \left|
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\mid S=s\right]
    -\mathbb{E}\!\left[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\right]
  \right|.
  \label{eq:supipm}
\end{align}
Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm}) is the representation-level counterpart of $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}$: it measures the largest discrepancy between the conditional and marginal distributions before a prediction head is chosen.

By Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm}), the constraint $\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)\le\rho$ for a fairness level $\rho\ge0$ bounds $\left|\mathbb{E}[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\mid S=s]-\mathbb{E}[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})]\right|$ by $\rho$ for every discriminator $v\in\mathcal{V}$ and every $s\in\mathcal{S}$.  For comparison with EIPM, write $\operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$ for Equation~(\ref{eq:eipm}) with $\mathbf{Z}$ replaced by $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}$.  Because an average never exceeds a supremum, $\operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)\le\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$, so a supIPM bound of $\rho$ implies an EIPM bound at the same fairness level.  The converse fails without further regularity in $s$.

It remains to choose the discriminator class $\mathcal{V}$.  Because $\mathcal{V}$ fixes the prediction heads that supIPM controls, a guarantee for every head of bounded H\"older norm calls for the H\"older ball $\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d$.  The H\"older-IPM cannot be computed directly, so we take $\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$, the ReLU discriminator class of Section~\ref{sec:relu-ipm}, for which the inner IPM in Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm}) is the ReLU-IPM.  The inequality in Equation~(\ref{eq:relu-holder}) applies to $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ and $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}$ at every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, so a bound on $\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$ implies a bound on $\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$.

\subsection{Estimation of supIPM}
\label{sec:supipm-estimation}

For the sample $\mathcal{D}$ of Section~\ref{sec:frem}, let $\mathbf{Z}_i=h_{\phi}(\mathbf{X}_i)$ and $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i=\mathbf{Z}_i/\sqrt{\lVert\mathbf{Z}_i\rVert_2^2+1}$.  Estimating supIPM requires the conditional distribution $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ at every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, whereas FREM requires it only at the observed values $S_i$.  We therefore adapt the kernel-weighted construction of Equation~(\ref{eq:frem-conditional}) to an arbitrary $s$.  Because $s$ is no longer tied to an observation, the leave-one-out correction is not needed, and all $n$ observations are used in both the conditional and the marginal estimates:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)
  :=\frac{K_{\gamma}(S_i,s)}{\sum_{j=1}^n K_{\gamma}(S_j,s)},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}
  :=\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)\,
  \delta_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}
  :=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i},
  \label{eq:supipm-estimators}
\end{equation}
where $K_{\gamma}$ is a kernel on $\mathcal{S}$ with bandwidth $\gamma>0$.  Observations whose sensitive values are close to $s$ receive larger weights.  The weights sum to one over $i$, so that $\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ is a probability distribution.  Substituting the two empirical distributions into Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm}) yields
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  (\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  \left(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s},
        \widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}\right) \notag\\
  &=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
    \sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\,\mu\in[-1,1]}
  \left|
    \sum_{i=1}^n
    \left(\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)-\frac{1}{n}\right)
    (\theta^\top\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i+\mu)_+
  \right|.
  \label{eq:empirical-supipm}
\end{align}
Whereas the EIPM estimator in Equation~(\ref{eq:eipm-estimator}) averages the discrepancy at the observed sensitive values, Equation~(\ref{eq:empirical-supipm}) takes the largest estimated discrepancy over all of $\mathcal{S}$.  When $K_{\gamma}$ is differentiable, the weights are differentiable in $s$, so gradient ascent over $\mathcal{S}$ can search for the largest discrepancy.  
% Section~\ref{sec:supipm-objective} performs this search during training.

\subsection{Fair Representation Learning with supIPM}
\label{sec:supipm-objective}
%s^* 찾는 알고리즘 수정 처음엔 0.3 0.5 0.7 중에 큰거 0.7이 뽑혔으면 이제 0.6 0.7 0.8 중에 큰거. 0.6이 뽑혔으면 0.55 0.6 0.65 중 큰거... 이런식으로 찾는 알고리즘으로 수정.
We use the empirical supIPM as a penalty and train the encoder $h_{\phi}$ and the prediction head $f_{\psi}$ jointly by minimizing
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\phi,\psi)
  :=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
  \ell\!\left(f_{\psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i),Y_i\right)
  +\lambda\,
  \widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  (\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S),
  \label{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is the supervised loss of Section~\ref{sec:frem} and $\lambda\ge0$ controls the accuracy--fairness trade-off.  Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}) is the Lagrangian relaxation of minimizing the empirical supervised risk subject to $\widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)\le\rho$ for a fairness level $\rho\ge0$, as in Section~\ref{sec:supipm-criterion}.

Minimizing Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}) requires evaluating the empirical supIPM in Equation~(\ref{eq:empirical-supipm}), which contains two suprema: an outer supremum over the sensitive value $s\in\mathcal{S}$ and an inner supremum over the discriminator parameter $(\theta,\mu)$.  For $s\in\mathcal{S}$ and $(\theta,\mu)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]$, let $\Delta(s;\theta,\mu)$ denote the discrepancy detected at $s$ by the discriminator $v_{\theta,\mu}$:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta(s;\theta,\mu)
  :=\left|
    \sum_{i=1}^n
    \left(\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)-\frac{1}{n}\right)
    (\theta^\top\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i+\mu)_+
  \right|.
  \label{eq:discriminator-discrepancy}
\end{equation}
Then $\widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\,\mu\in[-1,1]}\Delta(s;\theta,\mu)$, so the minimization of Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}) is a min--max problem, which we solve by alternating gradient descent in $(\phi,\psi)$ with projected gradient ascent in $s$ and in $(\theta,\mu)$.

Because gradient ascent can converge to a local maximum, we follow \citet{park2025relu} and perform the ascent from several initial values in parallel, maintaining $T$ candidate sensitive values $\mathbf{s}:=(s^{(1)},\ldots,s^{(T)})$ and $M$ discriminators $\mathbf{v}:=((\theta_1,\mu_1),\ldots,(\theta_M,\mu_M))$, where $(\theta_m,\mu_m)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]$ for $m\in[M]:=\{1,\ldots,M\}$.  Let $s^{\star}:=\argmax_{s\in\{s^{(1)},\ldots,s^{(T)}\}}\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s;\theta_m,\mu_m)$ be the candidate at which the largest discrepancy is detected.  The candidates and the discriminators are updated by gradient ascent on
\begin{equation}
  G(\mathbf{s}):=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s^{(t)};\theta_m,\mu_m),
  \qquad
  H(\mathbf{v}):=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Delta(s^{\star};\theta_m,\mu_m),
  \label{eq:stacked-objectives}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}$ and $s^{\star}$ are held fixed at their current values in $G$ and in $H$, respectively.  The $t$-th summand of $G$ depends on $\mathbf{s}$ only through $s^{(t)}$, and the $m$-th summand of $H$ depends on $\mathbf{v}$ only through $(\theta_m,\mu_m)$, so one ascent step on $G$ moves all $T$ candidates and one ascent step on $H$ moves all $M$ discriminators.  We write $\Pi_{A}$ for the projection onto a set $A$.  After each ascent step, $\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}$ returns each candidate to the sensitive domain, and $\Pi_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]}$ returns each $\theta_m$ to the unit sphere and each $\mu_m$ to $[-1,1]$.  The encoder and the prediction head are then updated by gradient descent on $\mathcal{L}(\phi,\psi)$ with the empirical supIPM replaced by $\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s^{\star};\theta_m,\mu_m)$, that is, with the outer supremum taken over the $T$ candidates and the inner supremum over the $M$ discriminators.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:supipm-frl} assembles these three updates into $R$ iterations, and we name the resulting procedure supIPM-FRL.  Each iteration performs $J_v$ discriminator updates, each preceded by $J_s$ ascent steps of freshly initialized candidates, and closes with one descent step.  The discriminator updates, the candidate ascent steps and the descent step use the step sizes $\eta_v$, $\eta_s$ and $\eta$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{supIPM-FRL}
\label{alg:supipm-frl}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE sample $\mathcal{D}$, supervised loss $\ell$, kernel $K_{\gamma}$ with bandwidth $\gamma$, penalty parameter $\lambda$, number of candidates $T$, number of discriminators $M$
\ENSURE encoder $h_{\phi}$ and prediction head $f_{\psi}$
\STATE Initialize $\phi$, $\psi$, and $\mathbf{v}$ with $(\theta_m,\mu_m)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]$ for $m\in[M]$
\FOR{$r=1,\ldots,R$}
  \FOR{$j=1,\ldots,J_v$}
    \STATE Initialize $\mathbf{s}$ at $T$ distinct points of $\mathcal{S}$ \label{alg:s-init}
    \FOR{$J_s$ steps}
      \STATE $\mathbf{s}\leftarrow\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}\!\left(\mathbf{s}+\eta_s\,\nabla_{\mathbf{s}}G(\mathbf{s})\right)$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE $s^{\star}\leftarrow\argmax_{s\in\{s^{(1)},\ldots,s^{(T)}\}}\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s;\theta_m,\mu_m)$ \label{alg:s-star}
    \STATE $\mathbf{v}\leftarrow\Pi_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]}\!\left(\mathbf{v}+\eta_v\,\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}H(\mathbf{v})\right)$
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE Recompute $s^{\star}$ by repeating lines~\ref{alg:s-init}--\ref{alg:s-star} with the updated $\mathbf{v}$
  \STATE $(\phi,\psi)\leftarrow(\phi,\psi)-\eta\,\nabla_{(\phi,\psi)}\big[\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\ell(f_{\psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i),Y_i)+\lambda\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s^{\star};\theta_m,\mu_m)\big]$ \label{alg:encoder-step}
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $h_{\phi}$, $f_{\psi}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Equations~(\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}) and (\ref{eq:discriminator-discrepancy}) are built from sample averages, so the procedure applies unchanged when these averages are computed on the current minibatch.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

In Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform} below we show that the empirical supIPM estimates its population counterpart uniformly over a class of encoders.  Corollary~\ref{cor:certificate} converts this estimate into a bound on the worst discrepancy of every prediction head of bounded H\"older norm.

Write $\mathcal{S}=[a,b]$ and let $\tau(\mathbf{z}):=(1+\lVert\mathbf{z}\rVert_2^2)^{-1/2}\mathbf{z}$ map $\mathbb{R}^d$ into $\mathbb{B}^d$.  For an encoder $h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^d$ put $\widetilde{h}:=\tau\circ h$, so that $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}=\tau(h_{\phi}(\mathbf{X}))$.  Both results are stated on the interior region $\mathcal{S}_n:=[a+\epsilon_n,\,b-\epsilon_n]$, where $0<\epsilon_n<\tfrac{1}{2}(b-a)$ and $\epsilon_n\to0$.  Restricting the supremum over $s$ in Equations~(\ref{eq:linfty-gdp}), (\ref{eq:supipm}) and (\ref{eq:empirical-supipm}) to $\mathcal{S}_n$ gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{GDP}_{\infty,n}(g)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}
     \left|\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s]-\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})]\right|,
  \\
  D_n(h)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}
     \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
     \bigl(\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s},
           \mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X})}\bigr),
  \\
  \widehat{D}_n(h)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}
     \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
     \bigl(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma_n}_{\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s},
           \widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X})}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:interior}
\end{equation}
Throughout, conditional expectations given $S=s$ are the versions in Equation~(\ref{eq:pointwise-conditional}), and $p(\mathbf{x},s)$ and $\nu$ are as in Section~\ref{sec:supipm-criterion}.  The bandwidth $\gamma_n$ is that of Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm-estimators}), and $\widehat{w}_{\gamma_n}(i;s)$ is set to zero when its denominator vanishes.

We impose three further conditions, stated in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} together with the proofs.  Assumption~\ref{as:smooth-density} requires independent copies of $(\mathbf{X},S,Y)$ and a joint density twice continuously differentiable in $s$ for $\nu$-almost every $\mathbf{x}$, with envelopes $M_1,M_2\in L_1(\nu)$ for its first two derivatives.  Assumption~\ref{as:kernel} specifies $K_{\gamma}(s',s)=\gamma^{-1}K((s'-s)/\gamma)$ with a bounded, symmetric and Lipschitz probability density $K$ of finite second moment.  The bandwidth satisfies $\gamma_n\to0$, $\gamma_n\le\min\{\epsilon_n,1/2\}$ and a tail condition that bounds the kernel mass beyond $\mathcal{S}$ by $\gamma_n^2$ at every $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$.  Assumption~\ref{as:encoder} bounds the covering number of the encoder class $\mathcal{E}_n$ in the supremum norm by $(A_n/\eta)^{\mathfrak{v}_n}$ for $0<\eta\le1$.  For $0<\delta<1$, let
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{n,\delta}
  :=(\mathfrak{v}_n+d)\log\frac{A_nn}{\gamma_n}+\log\frac{1}{\delta},
  \qquad
  r_{n,\delta}
  :=\gamma_n^2+\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{n,\delta}}{n\gamma_n}} .
  \label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform convergence of the empirical supIPM]
\label{thm:uniform}
Let Assumptions~\ref{as:positive-density}--\ref{as:encoder} hold.  There exist constants $C,c>0$, depending only on $c_S$, $C_S$, $K$, $\lVert M_1\rVert_{L_1(\nu)}$ and $\lVert M_2\rVert_{L_1(\nu)}$, such that for every $\delta\in(0,1)$ and every $n$ with $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\le c\,n\gamma_n$, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{h\in\mathcal{E}_n}
  \left|\widehat{D}_n(h)-D_n(h)\right|
  \ \le\ C\,r_{n,\delta} .
  \label{eq:uniform}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform} provides the convergence rate $r_{n,\delta}$ of the empirical supIPM.  The first term of $r_{n,\delta}$ is the bias of the local constant smoother in Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm-estimators}), of order $\gamma_n^2$.  The second term is a stochastic error of order $(n\gamma_n)^{-1/2}$ inflated by the entropy of $\mathcal{E}_n$.  The condition $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\le cn\gamma_n$ requires the effective sample size $n\gamma_n$ in a bandwidth window to dominate that entropy.  Because the encoder produced by Algorithm~\ref{alg:supipm-frl} is a function of the sample, the supremum over any class $\mathcal{E}_n$ containing it cannot be removed.  The ReLU discriminator class $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$ contributes only the $d+1$ coordinates of $(\theta,\mu)$ to the entropy, so the representation dimension enters $\Gamma_{n,\delta}$ linearly and leaves the order of $r_{n,\delta}$ in $n$ unchanged.

For $B>0$ let $\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d(B)$ be the H\"older ball of Equation~(\ref{eq:holder-ball}) with radius $B$.  Let $C_{d,\beta}$ and $\alpha_{d,\beta}$ be the constant and exponent in Equation~(\ref{eq:relu-holder}), with $\alpha_{d,\beta}=2\beta/(d+3)$ for $\beta<(d+3)/2$ and $1$ for $\beta>(d+3)/2$.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:certificate}
Let the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform} hold, let $d\ge3$, and let $C$ and $c$ be the constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform}.  For every $\delta\in(0,1)$ and  $n$ with $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\le c\,n\gamma_n$, with probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for all  $h\in\mathcal{E}_n$,  $B>0$ and  $\beta>0$ with $\beta\ne(d+3)/2$,
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{f\in\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d(B)}
  \operatorname{GDP}_{\infty,n}(f\circ\widetilde{h})
  \ \le\ C_{d,\beta}\,B
  \left\{\widehat{D}_n(h)+Cr_{n,\delta}\right\}^{\alpha_{d,\beta}} .
  \label{eq:certificate}
\end{equation}
If in addition $A_n$ and $\mathfrak{v}_n$ are bounded, $\delta:=\delta_n=n^{-c_1}$ for some $c_1>0$, $\gamma_n\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/5}$ and $\epsilon_n\asymp1/\log n$, then $r_{n,\delta_n}=O\{(\log n/n)^{2/5}\}$.
\end{corollary}

In Corollary~\ref{cor:certificate} the right-hand side of Equation~(\ref{eq:certificate}) depends on neither the sensitive value nor the prediction head, and the bound holds simultaneously over $\mathcal{E}_n$.  A single evaluation of $\widehat{D}_n$ therefore certifies every H\"older prediction head at every sensitive value in $\mathcal{S}_n$.  The exponent $\alpha_{d,\beta}$ is the cost of measuring the discrepancy through the parametric class $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$ rather than through the H\"older ball itself, and equals one when $\beta>(d+3)/2$.

\subsection{Assumptions}
\label{app:assumptions}

Assumption~\ref{as:positive-density} of Section~\ref{sec:supipm-criterion} is maintained throughout, with $p(\mathbf{x},s)$ and $\nu$ as fixed there, and $\mathcal{S}=[a,b]$, $\mathcal{S}_n$, $\epsilon_n$ and $\gamma_n$ as in Section~\ref{sec:theory}.  We now state the three conditions imposed there.

\begin{assumption}[Smooth joint density]
\label{as:smooth-density}
The sample $\mathcal{D}$ consists of independent copies of $(\mathbf{X},S,Y)$.  For $\nu$-almost every $\mathbf{x}$ the map $s\mapsto p(\mathbf{x},s)$ is twice continuously differentiable on $\mathcal{S}$, and there exist $M_1,M_2\in L_1(\nu)$ such that $\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\left|\partial_s^{j}p(\mathbf{x},s)\right|\le M_j(\mathbf{x})$ for $j\in\{1,2\}$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{as:smooth-density} imposes no smoothness in $\mathbf{x}$.  The envelopes $M_1$ and $M_2$ serve only to differentiate under the integral sign in $s$, uniformly over bounded measurable $g$.

\begin{assumption}[Kernel and bandwidth]
\label{as:kernel}
$K_{\gamma}(s',s)=\gamma^{-1}K((s'-s)/\gamma)$ for a bounded, symmetric and Lipschitz function $K:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)$ with $\int K=1$ and $\int t^2K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty$.  With $T_K(r):=\int_{|t|>r}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$ the tail mass of $K$, the bandwidth satisfies $\gamma_n\to0$, $0<\gamma_n\le\min\{\epsilon_n,1/2\}$ and $T_K(\epsilon_n/\gamma_n)\le\gamma_n^2$.
\end{assumption}

The tail condition of Assumption~\ref{as:kernel} bounds the kernel mass beyond $\mathcal{S}$ by $\gamma_n^2$ at every $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$.  It is vacuous for a kernel supported on $[-1,1]$.  For the Gaussian kernel it holds whenever $\epsilon_n\asymp1/\log n$ and $\gamma_n$ decays polynomially.

\begin{assumption}[Encoder class]
\label{as:encoder}
$\mathcal{E}_n$ is a pointwise measurable class \citep[Section~2.3.3]{vaart1996weak} of measurable encoders $h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^d$ such that, with $\lVert h-h'\rVert_\infty:=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\lVert h(\mathbf{x})-h'(\mathbf{x})\rVert_2$ and $N(\eta,\mathcal{F},\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)$ the smallest number of $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$-balls of radius $\eta$ covering a class $\mathcal{F}$, there exist $A_n\ge e$ and $\mathfrak{v}_n\ge1$ with $N(\eta,\mathcal{E}_n,\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)\le(A_n/\eta)^{\mathfrak{v}_n}$ for $0<\eta\le1$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{as:encoder} holds on a bounded $\mathcal{X}$ for ReLU networks of depth $L$ and width $H$ with bounded parameters.  For such networks $\mathfrak{v}_n$ is of the order of the number of parameters and $\log A_n$ of the order of $L\log H$ \citep{schmidthieber2020nonparametric}.

\subsection{Notation}
\label{app:notation}

Throughout the appendix $\mathbb{E}$ denotes expectation under the joint distribution of $(\mathbf{X},S)$ and $\mathbb{E}_n$ the average over the sample, $\mathbb{E}_n\varphi:=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\varphi(\mathbf{X}_i,S_i)$.  We abbreviate $\gamma:=\gamma_n$ and $\epsilon:=\epsilon_n$.  We write $C$ for a finite positive constant depending only on $c_S$, $C_S$, $\lVert M_1\rVert_{L_1(\nu)}$, $\lVert M_2\rVert_{L_1(\nu)}$ and $K$, whose value may change from one occurrence to the next.  Since $p_S$ integrates to one and is bounded below by $c_S$, the length $b-a\le1/c_S$ of $\mathcal{S}$ is controlled by the same list.

The encoder class $\mathcal{E}_n$ and the ReLU discriminator class $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$ induce the class
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{G}_n:=\left\{\mathbf{x}\mapsto v(\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{x})):
    h\in\mathcal{E}_n,\ v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}\right\} .
  \label{eq:score-class}
\end{equation}
Every $g\in\mathcal{G}_n$ satisfies $0\le g\le2$, because $0\le(\theta^\top\mathbf{z}+\mu)_+\le\lVert\theta\rVert_2\lVert\mathbf{z}\rVert_2+|\mu|\le2$ for $\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{B}^d$, $\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ and $\mu\in[-1,1]$.  For a measurable $g:\mathcal{X}\to[0,2]$ and $s\in\mathcal{S}$ let
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  a_g(s)&:=\int_{\mathcal{X}}g(\mathbf{x})p(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x}),
  &\qquad
  m_g(s)&:=\frac{a_g(s)}{p_S(s)}=\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s],
  \\
  b_{g,\gamma}(s)&:=\mathbb{E}\{K_{\gamma}(S,s)g(\mathbf{X})\},
  &\qquad
  q_{\gamma}(s)&:=\mathbb{E}K_{\gamma}(S,s),
  \\
  m_{g,\gamma}(s)&:=\frac{b_{g,\gamma}(s)}{q_{\gamma}(s)},
  &\qquad
  \widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)
  &:=\frac{\mathbb{E}_n\{K_{\gamma}(S,s)g(\mathbf{X})\}}{\mathbb{E}_nK_{\gamma}(S,s)},
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:app-notation}
\end{equation}
where $m_g$ is the version of the conditional expectation fixed in Equation~(\ref{eq:pointwise-conditional}), and $m_{g,\gamma}$ and $\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}$ are its population and empirical local constant approximations.  Since $\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)=K_{\gamma}(S_i,s)/\sum_{j=1}^nK_{\gamma}(S_j,s)$, we have $\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)=\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)g(\mathbf{X}_i)$ whenever the denominator is positive.  Hence, for every $h\in\mathcal{E}_n$,
\begin{equation}
  D_n(h)=\sup_{g,s}\left|m_g(s)-\mathbb{E}g\right|,
  \qquad
  \widehat{D}_n(h)=\sup_{g,s}\left|\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)-\mathbb{E}_ng\right| ,
  \label{eq:D-in-scores}
\end{equation}
where the suprema run over $g=v\circ\widetilde{h}$ with $v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$ and over $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$.  Because $g$, $m_g$, $m_{g,\gamma}$ and $\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}$ all take values in $[0,2]$, both quantities in Equation~(\ref{eq:D-in-scores}) lie in $[0,2]$.

We use Bernstein's inequality in the following form \citep[e.g.,][Chapter~2]{boucheron2013concentration}.  If $W_1,\ldots,W_n$ are independent copies of a random variable $W$ with $0\le W\le M$ and $\operatorname{Var}(W)\le\sigma^2$, then
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^nW_i-\mathbb{E}W\right|\ge u\right)
  \le2\exp\left(-\frac{nu^2}{2\sigma^2+2Mu/3}\right),
  \qquad u>0 .
  \label{eq:bernstein}
\end{equation}
We also use the elementary inequality
\begin{equation}
  \left|\ \sup_{t\in T}|F(t)|-\sup_{t\in T}|G(t)|\ \right|
  \le\sup_{t\in T}\left|F(t)-G(t)\right|,
  \label{eq:sup-diff}
\end{equation}
valid for any set $T$ and any bounded $F,G:T\to\mathbb{R}$: for every $t$, $|F(t)|\le|G(t)|+\sup_T|F-G|$, so $\sup_T|F|\le\sup_T|G|+\sup_T|F-G|$, and the roles of $F$ and $G$ may be exchanged.

\subsection{Auxiliary lemmas}
\label{app:lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[Lipschitz continuity and entropy of the score class]
\label{lem:entropy} 
$ \\$
\emph{(i)} The map $\tau$ is $1$-Lipschitz on $\mathbb{R}^d$.\\
\emph{(ii)} For $\mathbf{z},\mathbf{z}'\in\mathbb{B}^d$, $\theta,\theta'\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ and $\mu,\mu'\in[-1,1]$,
\[
  \left|v_{\theta,\mu}(\mathbf{z})-v_{\theta',\mu'}(\mathbf{z}')\right|
  \le\lVert\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}'\rVert_2+\lVert\theta-\theta'\rVert_2+|\mu-\mu'| .
\]
\emph{(iii)} Under Assumption~\ref{as:encoder},
\[
  N(\eta,\mathcal{G}_n,\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)
  \le\left(\frac{9A_n}{\eta}\right)^{\mathfrak{v}_n+d+1},
  \qquad 0<\eta\le1 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Write $u(\mathbf{z}):=(1+\lVert\mathbf{z}\rVert_2^2)^{1/2}$, so that $\tau(\mathbf{z})=\mathbf{z}/u(\mathbf{z})$.  Differentiating, $\partial\tau_i/\partial z_j=\delta_{ij}u^{-1}-z_iz_ju^{-3}$, that is
\[
  J(\mathbf{z})=u(\mathbf{z})^{-1}\left(I_d-\frac{\mathbf{z}\mathbf{z}^\top}{u(\mathbf{z})^2}\right).
\]
The matrix $J(\mathbf{z})$ is symmetric.  It has eigenvalue $u^{-1}(1-\lVert\mathbf{z}\rVert_2^2u^{-2})=u^{-3}$ in the direction $\mathbf{z}$ and eigenvalue $u^{-1}$ of multiplicity $d-1$ on the orthogonal complement of $\mathbf{z}$.  Since $u(\mathbf{z})\ge1$, all eigenvalues lie in $(0,1]$, so the operator norm of $J(\mathbf{z})$ is at most one for every $\mathbf{z}$.  As $\mathbb{R}^d$ is convex, the mean value inequality gives $\lVert\tau(\mathbf{z})-\tau(\mathbf{z}')\rVert_2\le\lVert\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}'\rVert_2$.

(ii) The map $t\mapsto(t)_+$ is $1$-Lipschitz on $\mathbb{R}$, so
\[
  \left|v_{\theta,\mu}(\mathbf{z})-v_{\theta',\mu'}(\mathbf{z}')\right|
  \le\left|\theta^\top\mathbf{z}+\mu-\theta'^\top\mathbf{z}'-\mu'\right|
  \le\left|\theta^\top(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}')\right|
    +\left|(\theta-\theta')^\top\mathbf{z}'\right|+|\mu-\mu'| ,
\]
and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality together with $\lVert\theta\rVert_2=1$ and $\lVert\mathbf{z}'\rVert_2\le1$ bounds the first two terms by $\lVert\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}'\rVert_2$ and $\lVert\theta-\theta'\rVert_2$.

(iii) Let $0<\eta\le1$.  Combining (i) and (ii), for $h,h'\in\mathcal{E}_n$,
\begin{equation}
  \lVert v_{\theta,\mu}\circ\widetilde{h}-v_{\theta',\mu'}\circ\widetilde{h}'\rVert_\infty
  \le\lVert h-h'\rVert_\infty+\lVert\theta-\theta'\rVert_2+|\mu-\mu'| .
  \label{eq:score-lipschitz}
\end{equation}
Take an $(\eta/2)$-net of $\mathcal{E}_n$ in $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$, of cardinality at most $(2A_n/\eta)^{\mathfrak{v}_n}$ by Assumption~\ref{as:encoder}.  Next take an $(\eta/4)$-net of $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ in $\lVert\cdot\rVert_2$, of cardinality at most $(1+8/\eta)^d\le(9/\eta)^d$ by the standard volumetric bound for subsets of the unit ball.  Finally take an $(\eta/4)$-net of $[-1,1]$, of cardinality at most $\lceil4/\eta\rceil\le5/\eta$.  By Equation~(\ref{eq:score-lipschitz}) the induced functions form an $\eta$-net of $\mathcal{G}_n$, and its cardinality is at most
\[
  \left(\frac{2A_n}{\eta}\right)^{\mathfrak{v}_n}
  \left(\frac{9}{\eta}\right)^{d}\frac{5}{\eta}
  \le\left(\frac{9A_n}{\eta}\right)^{\mathfrak{v}_n+d+1},
\]
because $A_n\ge e$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Regularity of $a_g$ and $p_S$]
\label{lem:regularity}
Let Assumptions~\ref{as:positive-density} and \ref{as:smooth-density} hold and let $g:\mathcal{X}\to[0,2]$ be measurable.  Then $a_g$ and $p_S$ are twice continuously differentiable on $\mathcal{S}$, with
\[
  a_g^{(j)}(s)=\int_{\mathcal{X}}g(\mathbf{x})\,\partial_s^{j}p(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x}),
  \qquad
  \lVert a_g^{(j)}\rVert_\infty\le2\lVert M_j\rVert_{L_1(\nu)},
  \qquad
  \lVert p_S^{(j)}\rVert_\infty\le\lVert M_j\rVert_{L_1(\nu)}
\]
for $j\in\{1,2\}$.  Moreover $0\le a_g\le2p_S\le2C_S$ on $\mathcal{S}$, and consequently $0\le m_g\le2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $s\in\mathcal{S}$ and let $t\ne0$ be such that $s+t\in\mathcal{S}$.  For $\nu$-almost every $\mathbf{x}$ the map $s\mapsto p(\mathbf{x},s)$ is differentiable on $\mathcal{S}$, so by the mean value theorem there is $\xi_{\mathbf{x},t}$ between $s$ and $s+t$ with
\[
  \left|g(\mathbf{x})\frac{p(\mathbf{x},s+t)-p(\mathbf{x},s)}{t}\right|
  =\left|g(\mathbf{x})\,\partial_sp(\mathbf{x},\xi_{\mathbf{x},t})\right|
  \le2M_1(\mathbf{x}) .
\]
Since $M_1\in L_1(\nu)$, dominated convergence applies as $t\to0$ and yields $a_g'(s)=\int g\,\partial_sp\,\mathrm{d}\nu$.  Repeating the argument with $\partial_sp$ in place of $p$ and $M_2$ in place of $M_1$ gives $a_g''(s)=\int g\,\partial_s^2p\,\mathrm{d}\nu$.  Continuity of $a_g''$ follows from the continuity of $s\mapsto\partial_s^2p(\mathbf{x},s)$ and a further application of dominated convergence with envelope $2M_2$.  The stated bounds on $\lVert a_g^{(j)}\rVert_\infty$ are immediate from $0\le g\le2$, and the assertions for $p_S$ are the case $g\equiv1$, for which the envelope is $M_j$ rather than $2M_j$.  Finally $0\le a_g(s)\le2\int p(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x})=2p_S(s)\le2C_S$ by Assumption~\ref{as:positive-density}, and dividing by $p_S(s)>0$ gives $0\le m_g\le2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform bias of the local constant approximation]
\label{lem:bias}
Let Assumptions~\ref{as:positive-density}--\ref{as:kernel} hold.  Then, for every $n$ and every $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$,
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac{3}{4}c_S\ \le\ q_{\gamma}(s)\ \le\ C_S,
  \qquad
  \left|q_{\gamma}(s)-p_S(s)\right|\le C\gamma^2 ,
  \label{eq:denominator-bias}
\end{equation}
and, uniformly over measurable $g:\mathcal{X}\to[0,2]$,
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}\left|b_{g,\gamma}(s)-a_g(s)\right|\le C\gamma^2,
  \qquad
  \sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}\left|m_{g,\gamma}(s)-m_g(s)\right|\le C\gamma^2 .
  \label{eq:numerator-bias}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Extend $p(\mathbf{x},\cdot)$ and $p_S$ by zero outside $\mathcal{S}$, so that $a_g$ and $p_S$ are defined on all of $\mathbb{R}$ and vanish off $\mathcal{S}$.  By Assumption~\ref{as:kernel} and the substitution $s'=s+\gamma t$,
\begin{equation}
  b_{g,\gamma}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(t)\,a_g(s+\gamma t)\,\mathrm{d}t,
  \qquad
  q_{\gamma}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(t)\,p_S(s+\gamma t)\,\mathrm{d}t .
  \label{eq:change-of-variable}
\end{equation}
Fix $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$, set $r_n:=\epsilon/\gamma$ and $I_n:=[-r_n,r_n]$.  Since $\gamma\le\epsilon$ we have $r_n\ge1$, and $s+\gamma t\in[s-\epsilon,s+\epsilon]\subseteq\mathcal{S}$ for every $t\in I_n$.

\emph{The denominator.}  By Lemma~\ref{lem:regularity} and Assumption~\ref{as:positive-density}, $0\le p_S\le C_S$ everywhere after the extension, so $q_{\gamma}(s)\le C_S\int K=C_S$.  In addition, $p_S\ge c_S$ on $\mathcal{S}$ gives
\[
  q_{\gamma}(s)\ \ge\ \int_{I_n}K(t)p_S(s+\gamma t)\,\mathrm{d}t
  \ \ge\ c_S\{1-T_K(r_n)\}
  \ \ge\ c_S(1-\gamma^2)\ \ge\ \tfrac{3}{4}c_S ,
\]
where the third inequality is the tail condition of Assumption~\ref{as:kernel} and the fourth uses $\gamma\le1/2$.

\emph{The numerator.}  Write $\int_{\mathbb{R}}=\int_{I_n}+\int_{I_n^c}$ in the first identity of Equation~(\ref{eq:change-of-variable}).  Since $0\le a_g\le2C_S$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:regularity},
\begin{equation}
  \left|\int_{I_n^c}K(t)a_g(s+\gamma t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right|
  \le2C_ST_K(r_n)\le2C_S\gamma^2 .
  \label{eq:tail-term}
\end{equation}
On $I_n$ the segment joining $s$ and $s+\gamma t$ is contained in $\mathcal{S}$, so Taylor's theorem and Lemma~\ref{lem:regularity} give
\[
  \left|a_g(s+\gamma t)-a_g(s)-\gamma t\,a_g'(s)\right|
  \le\frac{\gamma^2t^2}{2}\lVert a_g''\rVert_\infty
  \le\gamma^2t^2\lVert M_2\rVert_{L_1(\nu)},
  \qquad t\in I_n .
\]
Multiply by $K(t)$ and integrate over $I_n$.  Because $K$ and $I_n$ are symmetric about the origin, $\int_{I_n}tK(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=0$, and with $\int_{I_n}K=1-T_K(r_n)$ this gives
\[
  \left|\int_{I_n}K(t)a_g(s+\gamma t)\,\mathrm{d}t-a_g(s)\right|
  \le a_g(s)T_K(r_n)
    +\gamma^2\lVert M_2\rVert_{L_1(\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^2K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t .
\]
Adding Equation~(\ref{eq:tail-term}), and using $T_K(r_n)\le\gamma^2$, $a_g\le2C_S$ and $\int t^2K<\infty$, gives the first bound in Equation~(\ref{eq:numerator-bias}).  The same argument with $g\equiv1$ gives the second bound in Equation~(\ref{eq:denominator-bias}).

\emph{The ratio.}  Since $m_g=a_g/p_S$ and $m_{g,\gamma}=b_{g,\gamma}/q_{\gamma}$,
\[
  m_{g,\gamma}(s)-m_g(s)
  =\frac{b_{g,\gamma}(s)-a_g(s)}{q_{\gamma}(s)}
   +m_g(s)\,\frac{p_S(s)-q_{\gamma}(s)}{q_{\gamma}(s)} ,
\]
as is checked by expanding the right-hand side.  Since $|m_g|\le2$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:regularity} and $q_{\gamma}\ge3c_S/4$ by Equation~(\ref{eq:denominator-bias}), inserting the two bias bounds just proved yields the second bound in Equation~(\ref{eq:numerator-bias}).  None of the constants depends on $g$ or on $s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform stochastic error]
\label{lem:stochastic}
Let Assumptions~\ref{as:positive-density}--\ref{as:encoder} hold.  There exist constants $C_\ast,c>0$, depending only on $c_S$, $C_S$ and $K$, such that for every $\delta\in(0,1)$ and every $n$ with $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\le c\,n\gamma$ the event
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{A}_n:=\Bigg\{
  &\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_n}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}
   \left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})\{K_{\gamma}(S,s)g(\mathbf{X})\}\right|
   \le C_\ast\Lambda_n,\ \
   \sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}
   \left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})K_{\gamma}(S,s)\right|\le C_\ast\Lambda_n,
  \\
  &\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_n}\left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})g\right|\le C_\ast\Lambda_n
  \Bigg\}
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:good-event}
\end{equation}
satisfies $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_n)\ge1-\delta$, where $\Lambda_n:=\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{n,\delta}}{n\gamma}}$.  Moreover, on the event $\mathcal{A}_n$, 
\begin{equation}
\inf_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}\mathbb{E}_nK_{\gamma}(S,s)\ge c_S/2
\end{equation}
holds.

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Throughout, $c$ is taken small enough for the finitely many requirements below, all of which depend only on $c_S$, $C_S$ and $K$.  Since $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\ge1$ and $\gamma\le1$, the hypothesis $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\le cn\gamma$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \gamma\ \ge\ \frac{1}{cn},
  \qquad
  \Lambda_n\ \ge\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\ \ge\ \frac{1}{n},
  \qquad
  \Lambda_n\ \le\ \sqrt{c} .
  \label{eq:lambda-range}
\end{equation}

\emph{Step 1: the numerator class.}  Let $u:=C_1\Lambda_n$ with $C_1\ge1$ to be chosen, and assume $c\le C_1^{-2}$, so that $n^{-1}\le u\le1$ by Equation~(\ref{eq:lambda-range}).  Let
\[
  \zeta_g:=\frac{\gamma u}{8\lVert K\rVert_\infty},
  \qquad
  \zeta_s:=\frac{\gamma^2u}{16L_K},
\]
where $L_K$ is the Lipschitz constant of $K$.  Replace $\zeta_g$ by $\min\{\zeta_g,1\}$ if necessary, which only refines the following net.  Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy}(iii) then provides a $\zeta_g$-net $g_1,\ldots,g_{N_1}$ of $\mathcal{G}_n$ in $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$ with $0\le g_k\le2$.  Let $s_1,\ldots,s_{N_2}$ be a $\zeta_s$-net of $\mathcal{S}_n$ in $|\cdot|$.  Given $g\in\mathcal{G}_n$ and $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$, choose $k$ and $j$ with $\lVert g-g_k\rVert_\infty\le\zeta_g$ and $|s-s_j|\le\zeta_s$.  Since $K$ is $L_K$-Lipschitz,
\[
  \left|K_{\gamma}(s',s)-K_{\gamma}(s',s_j)\right|
  =\frac{1}{\gamma}\left|K\!\left(\frac{s'-s}{\gamma}\right)
    -K\!\left(\frac{s'-s_j}{\gamma}\right)\right|
  \le\frac{L_K}{\gamma^2}|s-s_j| ,
\]
so, using $K_{\gamma}\le\lVert K\rVert_\infty/\gamma$ and $0\le g_k\le2$,
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X},\,s'\in\mathcal{S}}
  \left|K_{\gamma}(s',s)g(\mathbf{x})-K_{\gamma}(s',s_j)g_k(\mathbf{x})\right|
  \le\frac{\lVert K\rVert_\infty}{\gamma}\zeta_g+\frac{2L_K}{\gamma^2}\zeta_s
  \le\frac{u}{4} .
  \label{eq:discretisation}
\end{equation}
Applying Equation~(\ref{eq:discretisation}) under $\mathbb{E}_n$ and under $\mathbb{E}$ gives
\[
  \left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})\{K_{\gamma}(S,s)g(\mathbf{X})\}\right|
  \le\left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})\{K_{\gamma}(S,s_j)g_k(\mathbf{X})\}\right|
    +\frac{u}{2} ,
\]
and therefore, with the suprema running over $g\in\mathcal{G}_n$ and $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{P}\left(
    \sup_{g,s}
    \left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})\{K_{\gamma}(S,s)g(\mathbf{X})\}\right|>u
  \right)
  \le\sum_{k=1}^{N_1}\sum_{j=1}^{N_2}
    \mathbb{P}\left(
      \left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})\{K_{\gamma}(S,s_j)g_k(\mathbf{X})\}\right|
      >\tfrac{u}{2}\right).
  \label{eq:union-bound}
\end{equation}
The variable $W:=K_{\gamma}(S,s_j)g_k(\mathbf{X})$ satisfies $0\le W\le M:=2\lVert K\rVert_\infty/\gamma$ and, by $K_{\gamma}\le\lVert K\rVert_\infty/\gamma$ and $q_{\gamma}\le C_S$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:bias},
\[
  \operatorname{Var}(W)\le\mathbb{E}W^2
  \le\frac{4\lVert K\rVert_\infty}{\gamma}\,\mathbb{E}K_{\gamma}(S,s_j)
  \le\frac{4\lVert K\rVert_\infty C_S}{\gamma}=:\sigma^2 .
\]
By Equation~(\ref{eq:bernstein}) at $u/2$, each summand in Equation~(\ref{eq:union-bound}) is at most $2\exp\{-nu^2/(8\sigma^2+4Mu/3)\}$.  The right-hand side of Equation~(\ref{eq:union-bound}) is therefore bounded by $\delta/3$, provided that
\begin{equation}
  \frac{nu^2}{8\sigma^2+4Mu/3}\ \ge\ \ell,
  \qquad
  \ell:=\log\frac{6N_1N_2}{\delta} .
  \label{eq:bernstein-requirement}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:bernstein-requirement}) holds whenever $nu^2\ge16\ell\sigma^2$ and $nu^2\ge8\ell Mu/3$, that is whenever
\begin{equation}
  u\ \ge\ 8\sqrt{\frac{\lVert K\rVert_\infty C_S\ell}{n\gamma}}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  u\ \ge\ \frac{16\lVert K\rVert_\infty\ell}{3n\gamma} .
  \label{eq:u-requirement}
\end{equation}
It remains to bound $\ell$.  (iii) of Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy} with $\eta=\zeta_g$ and the bound $N_2\le1+(b-a)/(2\zeta_s)$ give
\[
  \log N_1\le(\mathfrak{v}_n+d+1)
    \log\frac{72\lVert K\rVert_\infty A_n}{\gamma u},
  \qquad
  \log N_2\le\log\left(1+\frac{8L_K(b-a)}{\gamma^2u}\right).
\]
Since $u\ge n^{-1}$, $\gamma\ge1/(cn)$, $b-a\le1/c_S$, $A_n\ge e$, $\mathfrak{v}_n\ge1$ and $d\ge1$, both logarithms are bounded by a multiple of $\log(A_nn/\gamma)$ with a factor depending only on $c_S$ and $K$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
  \ell\ \le\ C_0\left\{(\mathfrak{v}_n+d)\log\frac{A_nn}{\gamma}+\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right\}
  \ =\ C_0\,\Gamma_{n,\delta}
  \label{eq:entropy-bound}
\end{equation}
for a constant $C_0$ depending only on $c_S$ and $K$.  Since $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\le cn\gamma$, we have $\Gamma_{n,\delta}/(n\gamma)\le\sqrt{c}\,\Lambda_n$.  Both requirements in Equation~(\ref{eq:u-requirement}) are therefore met by $u=C_1\Lambda_n$ once $C_1$ is a large enough multiple of $\max\{\sqrt{\lVert K\rVert_\infty C_SC_0},\,\lVert K\rVert_\infty C_0\}$ and $c\le1$.  Fixing such a $C_1$ and then $c\le C_1^{-2}$ completes Step~1: the first bound in Equation~(\ref{eq:good-event}) fails with probability at most $\delta/3$.

\emph{Step 2: the denominator and the marginal.}  The class $\{s'\mapsto K_{\gamma}(s',s):s\in\mathcal{S}_n\}$ is handled by the same argument with $N_1=1$, where the $\zeta_s$-net alone controls the discretization error.  This gives the second bound in Equation~(\ref{eq:good-event}) with probability at least $1-\delta/3$.  For the class $\mathcal{G}_n$ the variables $W:=g_k(\mathbf{X})$ satisfy $0\le W\le2$ and $\operatorname{Var}(W)\le1$.  Only the $\zeta_g$-net is needed, with $\zeta_g$ replaced by $u/4$.  The same computation, together with $\gamma\le1$, gives the third bound with probability at least $1-\delta/3$.  A union bound over the three events yields $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_n)\ge1-\delta$, where $C_\ast$ is the largest of the three constants $C_1$ obtained.

\emph{Step 3: the denominator is bounded away from zero.}  On $\mathcal{A}_n$, for $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$,
\[
  \mathbb{E}_nK_{\gamma}(S,s)
  \ \ge\ q_{\gamma}(s)-C_\ast\Lambda_n
  \ \ge\ \frac{3c_S}{4}-C_\ast\sqrt{c}
  \ \ge\ \frac{c_S}{2}
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:bias} and Equation~(\ref{eq:lambda-range}), provided $c$ is small enough that $C_\ast\sqrt{c}\le c_S/4$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform}}
\label{app:proof-uniform}

\begin{proof}
Let $C_\ast$ and $c$ be the constants of Lemma~\ref{lem:stochastic} and let $\mathcal{A}_n$ be the event in Equation~(\ref{eq:good-event}), so that $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_n)\ge1-\delta$ whenever $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\le cn\gamma$.  We show that Equation~(\ref{eq:uniform}) holds on $\mathcal{A}_n$.

On $\mathcal{A}_n$ fix $g\in\mathcal{G}_n$ and $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:stochastic} the denominator $\mathbb{E}_nK_{\gamma}(S,s)$ is at least $c_S/2$, so $\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)$ is well defined and the zero convention for $\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)$ in Section~\ref{sec:theory} is never invoked.  Write
\[
  A_{g,s}:=(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})\{K_{\gamma}(S,s)g(\mathbf{X})\},
  \qquad
  B_s:=(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})K_{\gamma}(S,s) .
\]
Since $\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}=(b_{g,\gamma}+A_{g,s})/(q_{\gamma}+B_s)$ and $m_{g,\gamma}=b_{g,\gamma}/q_{\gamma}$, clearing denominators gives the identity
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)-m_{g,\gamma}(s)
  =\frac{A_{g,s}-m_{g,\gamma}(s)B_s}{\mathbb{E}_nK_{\gamma}(S,s)} .
  \label{eq:ratio-identity}
\end{equation}
Since $0\le g\le2$ and $K_{\gamma}\ge0$, we have $0\le b_{g,\gamma}(s)\le2q_{\gamma}(s)$ and hence $0\le m_{g,\gamma}(s)\le2$.  Equation~(\ref{eq:ratio-identity}), the bound $\mathbb{E}_nK_{\gamma}(S,s)\ge c_S/2$ and the first two bounds in Equation~(\ref{eq:good-event}) then give
\[
  \left|\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)-m_{g,\gamma}(s)\right|
  \le\frac{2}{c_S}\left(|A_{g,s}|+2|B_s|\right)
  \le\frac{6C_\ast}{c_S}\Lambda_n .
\]
Combining this with the bias bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:bias} and with the definition of $r_{n,\delta}$ in Equation~(\ref{eq:rate}),
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_n}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}
  \left|\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)-m_g(s)\right|
  \le C\gamma^2+\frac{6C_\ast}{c_S}\Lambda_n
  \le C\,r_{n,\delta} .
  \label{eq:conditional-mean-uniform}
\end{equation}

Now fix $h\in\mathcal{E}_n$.  Apply Equation~(\ref{eq:sup-diff}) with index set $T=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}\times\mathcal{S}_n$ and, for $t=(v,s)$ and $g=v\circ\widetilde{h}\in\mathcal{G}_n$,
\[
  F(t):=\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)-\mathbb{E}_ng,
  \qquad
  G(t):=m_g(s)-\mathbb{E}g ,
\]
both bounded by $2$ in absolute value.  By Equation~(\ref{eq:D-in-scores}), $\sup_T|F|=\widehat{D}_n(h)$ and $\sup_T|G|=D_n(h)$, so
\[
  \left|\widehat{D}_n(h)-D_n(h)\right|
  \le\sup_{t\in T}|F(t)-G(t)|
  \le\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_n}\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}
     \left|\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)-m_g(s)\right|
    +\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_n}\left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})g\right| .
\]
The first term is at most $Cr_{n,\delta}$ by Equation~(\ref{eq:conditional-mean-uniform}) and the second at most $C_\ast\Lambda_n\le C_\ast r_{n,\delta}$ by Equation~(\ref{eq:good-event}).  The resulting bound does not depend on $h$, so it also bounds $\sup_{h\in\mathcal{E}_n}\left|\widehat{D}_n(h)-D_n(h)\right|$.  This proves Equation~(\ref{eq:uniform}) on $\mathcal{A}_n$, and hence with probability at least $1-\delta$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:certificate}}
\label{app:proof-certificate}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal{A}_n$ be the event of Lemma~\ref{lem:stochastic}, on which Equation~(\ref{eq:uniform}) holds for every $h\in\mathcal{E}_n$ simultaneously, so that
\begin{equation}
  D_n(h)\le\widehat{D}_n(h)+Cr_{n,\delta},
  \qquad h\in\mathcal{E}_n .
  \label{eq:population-from-empirical}
\end{equation}
Fix $h\in\mathcal{E}_n$, $B>0$, $\beta>0$ with $\beta\ne(d+3)/2$, and $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$.  Write $\mathbb{P}_s:=\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s}$ and $\mathbb{P}_0:=\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X})}$, which are probability measures on $\mathbb{B}^d$ because $\tau$ takes values in $\mathbb{B}^d$.  For $f\in\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d(B)$ we have $f/B\in\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d$, and since $d\ge3$ and $\beta\ne(d+3)/2$, Theorem~2 of \citet{park2025relu}, restated in Equation~(\ref{eq:relu-holder}), gives
\begin{equation}
  \left|\mathbb{E}\!\left[f(\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X}))\mid S=s\right]
        -\mathbb{E}\!\left[f(\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X}))\right]\right|
  \le B\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}(\mathbb{P}_s,\mathbb{P}_0)
  \le C_{d,\beta}B\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
     (\mathbb{P}_s,\mathbb{P}_0)^{\alpha_{d,\beta}} .
  \label{eq:pointwise-transfer}
\end{equation}
The map $t\mapsto t^{\alpha_{d,\beta}}$ is nondecreasing on $[0,\infty)$, and $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P}_s,\mathbb{P}_0)\le D_n(h)$ by Equation~(\ref{eq:interior}).  The right-hand side of Equation~(\ref{eq:pointwise-transfer}) is therefore at most $C_{d,\beta}BD_n(h)^{\alpha_{d,\beta}}$, which depends on neither $s$ nor $f$.  Taking the supremum over $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$ and then over $f\in\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d(B)$ bounds the left-hand side of Equation~(\ref{eq:certificate}) by $C_{d,\beta}BD_n(h)^{\alpha_{d,\beta}}$.  Equation~(\ref{eq:population-from-empirical}) and the same monotonicity then give Equation~(\ref{eq:certificate}).  The event $\mathcal{A}_n$ depends on neither $h$, nor $B$, nor $\beta$, so the bound holds simultaneously for all of them.

For the rate, the boundedness of $A_n$ and $\mathfrak{v}_n$, together with $\delta_n=n^{-c_1}$, gives $\Gamma_{n,\delta_n}\asymp\log(n/\gamma_n)\asymp\log n$ for $\gamma_n\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/5}$.  With this bandwidth,
\[
  \gamma_n^2\asymp\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{2/5},
  \qquad
  \sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{n,\delta_n}}{n\gamma_n}}
  \asymp\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n^{4/5}(\log n)^{1/5}}}
  =\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{2/5},
\]
so $r_{n,\delta_n}=O\{(\log n/n)^{2/5}\}$.  The condition $\Gamma_{n,\delta_n}\le cn\gamma_n$ reads $\log n=O\{n^{4/5}(\log n)^{1/5}\}$, and $\gamma_n\le\epsilon_n$ reads $(\log n/n)^{1/5}=O(1/\log n)$.  Both hold for all large $n$.
\end{proof}

\section{Submission of conference papers to ICLR 2027}

ICLR requires electronic submissions, processed by
\url{https://openreview.net/}. See ICLR's website for more instructions.

If your paper is ultimately accepted, the statement {\tt
  {\textbackslash}iclrfinalcopy} should be inserted to adjust the
format to the camera ready requirements.

The format for the submissions is a variant of the NeurIPS format.
Please read carefully the instructions below, and follow them
faithfully.

\subsection{Style}

Papers to be submitted to ICLR 2027 must be prepared according to the
instructions presented here.

%% Please note that we have introduced automatic line number generation
%% into the style file for \LaTeXe. This is to help reviewers
%% refer to specific lines of the paper when they make their comments. Please do
%% NOT refer to these line numbers in your paper as they will be removed from the
%% style file for the final version of accepted papers.

Authors are required to use the ICLR \LaTeX{} style files obtainable at the
ICLR website. Please make sure you use the current files and
not previous versions. Tweaking the style files may be grounds for rejection.

\subsection{Retrieval of style files}

The style files for ICLR and other conference information are available online at:
\begin{center}
   \url{http://www.iclr.cc/}
\end{center}
The file \verb+iclr2027_conference.pdf+ contains these
instructions and illustrates the
various formatting requirements your ICLR paper must satisfy.
Submissions must be made using \LaTeX{} and the style files
\verb+iclr2027_conference.sty+ and \verb+iclr2027_conference.bst+ (to be used with \LaTeX{}2e). The file
\verb+iclr2027_conference.tex+ may be used as a ``shell'' for writing your paper. All you
have to do is replace the author, title, abstract, and text of the paper with
your own.

The formatting instructions contained in these style files are summarized in
sections \ref{gen_inst}, \ref{headings}, and \ref{others} below.

\section{General formatting instructions}
\label{gen_inst}

The text must be confined within a rectangle 5.5~inches (33~picas) wide and
9~inches (54~picas) long. The left margin is 1.5~inch (9~picas).
Use 10~point type with a vertical spacing of 11~points. Times New Roman is the
preferred typeface throughout. Paragraphs are separated by 1/2~line space,
with no indentation.

Paper title is 17~point, in small caps and left-aligned.
All pages should start at 1~inch (6~picas) from the top of the page.

Authors' names are
set in boldface, and each name is placed above its corresponding
address. The lead author's name is to be listed first, and
the co-authors' names are set to follow. Authors sharing the
same address can be on the same line.

Please pay special attention to the instructions in section \ref{others}
regarding figures, tables, acknowledgments, and references.


There will be a strict upper limit of \textbf{9 pages} for the main text of the initial submission, with unlimited additional pages for citations. This limit will be expanded to \textbf{10 pages} for rebuttal/camera ready.

\section{Headings: first level}
\label{headings}

First level headings are in small caps,
flush left and in point size 12. One line space before the first level
heading and 1/2~line space after the first level heading.

\subsection{Headings: second level}

Second level headings are in small caps,
flush left and in point size 10. One line space before the second level
heading and 1/2~line space after the second level heading.

\subsubsection{Headings: third level}

Third level headings are in small caps,
flush left and in point size 10. One line space before the third level
heading and 1/2~line space after the third level heading.

\section{Citations, figures, tables, references}
\label{others}

These instructions apply to everyone, regardless of the formatter being used.

\subsection{Citations within the text}

Citations within the text should be based on the \texttt{natbib} package
and include the authors' last names and year (with the ``et~al.'' construct
for more than two authors). When the authors or the publication are
included in the sentence, the citation should not be in parenthesis using \verb|\citet{}| (as
in ``See \citet{Hinton06} for more information.''). Otherwise, the citation
should be in parenthesis using \verb|\citep{}| (as in ``Deep learning shows promise to make progress
towards AI~\citep{Bengio+chapter2007}.'').

The corresponding references are to be listed in alphabetical order of
authors, in the \textsc{References} section. As to the format of the
references themselves, any style is acceptable as long as it is used
consistently.

\subsection{Footnotes}

Indicate footnotes with a number\footnote{Sample of the first footnote} in the
text. Place the footnotes at the bottom of the page on which they appear.
Precede the footnote with a horizontal rule of 2~inches
(12~picas).\footnote{Sample of the second footnote}

\subsection{Figures}

All artwork must be neat, clean, and legible. Lines should be dark
enough for purposes of reproduction; art work should not be
hand-drawn. The figure number and caption always appear after the
figure. Place one line space before the figure caption, and one line
space after the figure. The figure caption is lower case (except for
first word and proper nouns); figures are numbered consecutively.

Make sure the figure caption does not get separated from the figure.
Leave sufficient space to avoid splitting the figure and figure caption.

You may use color figures.
However, it is best for the
figure captions and the paper body to make sense if the paper is printed
either in black/white or in color.

All tables must be centered, neat, clean and legible. Do not use hand-drawn
tables. The table number and title always appear before the table. See

title, and one line space after the table. The table title must be lower case
(except for first word and proper nouns); tables are numbered consecutively.

\section{Final instructions}
Do not change any aspects of the formatting parameters in the style files.
In particular, do not modify the width or length of the rectangle the text
should fit into, and do not change font sizes (except perhaps in the
\textsc{References} section; see below). Please note that pages should be
numbered.

\section{Preparing PostScript or PDF files}

Please prepare PostScript or PDF files with paper size ``US Letter'', and
not, for example, ``A4''. The -t
letter option on dvips will produce US Letter files.

Consider directly generating PDF files using \verb+pdflatex+
(especially if you are a MiKTeX user).
PDF figures must be substituted for EPS figures, however.

Otherwise, please generate your PostScript and PDF files with the following commands:
\begin{verbatim}
dvips mypaper.dvi -t letter -Ppdf -G0 -o mypaper.ps
ps2pdf mypaper.ps mypaper.pdf
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Margins in LaTeX}

Most of the margin problems come from figures positioned by hand using
\verb+\special+ or other commands. We suggest using the command
\verb+\includegraphics+
from the graphicx package. Always specify the figure width as a multiple of
the line width as in the example below using .eps graphics
\begin{verbatim}
   \usepackage[dvips]{graphicx} ...
   \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{myfile.eps}
\end{verbatim}
or % Apr 2009 addition
\begin{verbatim}
   \usepackage[pdftex]{graphicx} ...
   \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{myfile.pdf}
\end{verbatim}
for .pdf graphics.
See section~4.4 in the graphics bundle documentation (\url{http://www.ctan.org/tex-archive/macros/latex/required/graphics/grfguide.ps})

A number of width problems arise when LaTeX cannot properly hyphenate a
line. Please give LaTeX hyphenation hints using the \verb+\-+ command.

\subsection*{Ethics statement}

(This section is \textbf{recommended} and does not count toward the page limit.)

If authors feel that their paper submission raises questions regarding the Code
of Ethics, they are encouraged to include a paragraph of Ethics Statement (at
the end of the main text before references) to address potential concerns where
appropriate. Topics include, but are not limited to, studies that involve human
subjects, practices to data set releases, potentially harmful insights,
methodologies and applications, potential conflicts of interest and sponsorship,
discrimination/bias/fairness concerns, privacy and security issues, legal
compliance, and research integrity issues (e.g., IRB, documentation, research
ethics). This statement should not be more than 1 page.

\subsection*{Reproducibility statement}

(This section is \textbf{recommended} and does not count toward the page limit.)

It is important that the work published in ICLR is reproducible. Authors are
strongly encouraged to include a paragraph-long Reproducibility Statement at the
end of the main text (before references) to discuss the efforts that have been
made to ensure reproducibility. This paragraph should not itself describe
details needed for reproducing the results, but rather reference the parts of
the main paper, appendix, and supplemental materials that will help with
reproducibility. For example, for novel models or algorithms, a link to an
anonymous downloadable source code can be submitted as supplementary materials;
for theoretical results, clear explanations of any assumptions and a complete
proof of the claims can be included in the appendix; for any datasets used in
the experiments, a complete description of the data processing steps can be
provided in the supplementary materials. Each of the above are examples of
things that can be referenced in the reproducibility statement.

\subsubsection*{Author Contributions}
If you'd like to, you may include  a section for author contributions as is done
in many journals. This is optional and at the discretion of the authors.

\subsubsection*{Acknowledgments}
Use unnumbered third level headings for the acknowledgments. All
acknowledgments, including those to funding agencies, go at the end of the paper.
