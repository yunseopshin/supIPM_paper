\begin{abstract}
Fair representation learning (FRL) seeks a representation that is informative for downstream prediction yet independent of a sensitive attribute. For a continuous sensitive attribute, existing methods measure the discrepancy between conditional and marginal distributions at each sensitive value and \emph{average} it over the distribution of the sensitive attribute. Such an average can be small even when a large discrepancy is confined to a low-probability region, leaving some subpopulations unprotected. We propose $L_\infty$-generalized demographic parity, which replaces the average by a supremum, and its representation-level counterpart, the supremum integral probability metric (supIPM). We then propose supIPM-FRL, which learns a representation under a constraint on the empirical supIPM. The procedure estimates conditional distributions by kernel weighting and measures their discrepancy from the marginal distribution by ReLU-IPM. 
% Such a discriminator is determined by a direction and a bias alone, so each discrepancy is maximized over the discriminator class by gradient ascent. 
We prove that the empirical supIPM is a uniformly consistent estimator, so the fairness level attained on the sample transfers to the population. 
% The transfer is uniform over the sensitive domain and over prediction heads of bounded H\"older norm. 
Experiments on tabular and graph benchmarks show that, at every fairness level, supIPM-FRL attains prediction performance comparable to existing methods while substantially reducing the worst-case discrepancy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A learned representation serves many downstream tasks, including tasks that are unspecified when the representation is trained \citep{bengio2013representation}.  This transferability makes the fairness requirement more demanding, because constraining one prediction head does not prevent another from exploiting sensitive information retained in the representation \citep{madras2018adversarially}.  Fair representation learning (FRL) addresses this problem by seeking a representation that is informative for prediction and independent of the sensitive attribute $S$ \citep{zemel2013learning}.
%, so that every measurable function of the representation is again independent of $S$


Most existing FRL methods have been developed for a categorical $S$.  In this case, demographic parity (DP) requires the prediction to have the same distribution in every group, and these methods enforce DP by matching the distribution of the representation across groups.  One line of work trains an adversary to recover group membership from the representation \citep{edwards2016censoring,madras2018adversarially}.  Another compares the distribution of the representation across groups through the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) of a fixed kernel \citep{louizos2016variational}, an integral probability metric (IPM) over a prespecified parametric discriminator class \citep{kim2022parametric}, or the power of an MMD two-sample test with a learned kernel \citep{deka2023mmdbfair}.  This groupwise construction does not extend directly to a continuous $S$ such as age or income.  Distinct observations take distinct sensitive values with probability one, so at most one observation is available at any given sensitive value $s$, and one observation does not suffice to estimate the conditional distribution of the representation at $S=s$.  Binning $S$ restores the groupwise construction, but the resulting criterion depends on the choice of cutpoints \citep{kong2025fair}.  The discretization also introduces threshold effects and discards the ordering of the sensitive values \citep{mary2019continuous}.

Existing methods that treat $S$ as continuous have largely focused on the fitted predictor rather than on the representation, measuring its dependence on $S$ through maximal correlation or through generalized demographic parity (GDP) \citep{mary2019continuous,grari2020continuous,jiang2022gdp}.  Of these, GDP is the average over $S$ of the absolute difference between the conditional and marginal mean predictions.  \citet{kong2025fair} apply the same average at the representation level: the expectation of integral probability metrics (EIPM) is the average over $S$ of the IPM between the conditional distribution of the representation given $S=s$ and its marginal distribution.  Both criteria average the discrepancy over the distribution of $S$, so a large discrepancy confined to a low-probability range of sensitive values contributes little to the average.  The average thus departs from categorical DP, which imposes one fairness level on every group regardless of its probability.

A new criterion for a continuous $S$ should therefore hold at every sensitive value.  For a representation, the criterion should also hold for every prediction head that may later be fitted on it.  In this paper we propose criteria that take the supremum over $S$ in place of the average, so that a single bound holds uniformly over the sensitive domain.  This replacement transforms GDP into $L_{\infty}$-generalized demographic parity ($L_{\infty}$-GDP) and EIPM into the supremum integral probability metric (supIPM).  Our estimator of supIPM obtains the conditional distribution at an arbitrary $s$ from kernel weights, without binning, and searches the sensitive domain for the largest discrepancy from the marginal distribution.  The discrepancy is measured by the ReLU-IPM of \citet{park2025relu}, whose discriminators are single rectified linear units (ReLU) and which controls every prediction head of bounded H\"older norm.  We add this supIPM estimate as a penalty to the supervised risk and train the encoder and the prediction head with minibatches.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We introduce $L_{\infty}$-GDP, a fairness criterion for prediction models, and supIPM, its representation-level counterpart.  Both extend the groupwise requirement of categorical DP to a continuous sensitive attribute by measuring the worst-case discrepancy over the sensitive domain.
  \item We estimate supIPM from kernel weights and ReLU-IPM, and propose supIPM-FRL, an adversarial procedure that is compatible with minibatch training.
  \item We prove that the empirical supIPM is a uniformly consistent estimator, so that the fairness level attained on the sample transfers to the population.
  % uniformly over the sensitive domain and over prediction heads of bounded H\"older norm.
\end{itemize}

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

\subsection{Fair representation using EIPM (FREM)}
\label{sec:frem}

We observe an input $\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{X}$, an outcome $Y\in\mathcal{Y}$, and a continuous sensitive attribute $S$ taking values in the sensitive domain $\mathcal{S}$, a compact interval of $\mathbb{R}$.  An encoder $h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}$ maps $\mathbf{X}$ to a representation $\mathbf{Z}=h(\mathbf{X})$ in the representation space $\mathcal{Z}=\mathbb{R}^d$, where $d$ is the representation dimension.  A prediction head $f:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}$ maps the representation to a real-valued prediction, and $f\circ h$ denotes the composed prediction model.  When the encoder and the prediction head are parameterized, we write them as $h_{\phi}$ and $f_{\psi}$ with parameters $\phi$ and $\psi$.  We write $\mathbb{P}_S$ for the distribution of $S$, $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ for the marginal distribution of $\mathbf{Z}$, and $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}$ for the conditional distribution of $\mathbf{Z}$ given $S=s$.  
% Equality $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ for $\mathbb{P}_S$-almost every $s$ is equivalent to independence of $\mathbf{Z}$ and $S$, and this independence is inherited by every measurable function of $\mathbf{Z}$.

The discrepancy between $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}$ and $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ is measured through a discriminator class $\mathcal{V}$, a class of real-valued discriminator functions on $\mathcal{Z}$. The IPM associated with $\mathcal{V}$ between two probability measures $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})
  :=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
  \left|\int v(\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{z})-\int v(\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{z})\right|.
  \label{eq:prelim-ipm}
\end{equation}
We say that $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}$ separates probability distributions if $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})=0$ implies $\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{Q}$.  The class $\mathcal{V}$ determines which prediction heads the IPM controls, because $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})$ bounds $\left|\int v\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}-\int v\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}\right|$ for every $v\in\mathcal{V}$, including any prediction head in $\mathcal{V}$.

For a continuous sensitive attribute, \citet{kong2025fair} propose fair representation using EIPM (FREM).  They average, over the distribution of $S$, the IPM between the conditional and the marginal distribution, and define EIPM as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)
  :=\int_{\mathcal{S}}
    \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s},\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}})
  \,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_S(s).
  \label{eq:eipm}
\end{equation}
If $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}$ separates probability distributions, then $\operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)=0$ implies $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ for $\mathbb{P}_S$-almost every $s$.

FREM estimates the conditional distribution at each observed $S_i$ by kernel weighting.  Given the sample $\mathcal{D}=\{(\mathbf{X}_i,S_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ and $\mathbf{Z}_i=h(\mathbf{X}_i)$, FREM defines
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i),\gamma}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=S_i}
  :=\sum_{j\ne i}\widehat{w}^{(-i)}_{\gamma}(j)\,\delta_{\mathbf{Z}_j},
  \qquad
  \widehat{w}^{(-i)}_{\gamma}(j)
  :=\frac{K_{\gamma}(S_j,S_i)}{\sum_{r\ne i}K_{\gamma}(S_r,S_i)},
  \label{eq:frem-conditional}
\end{equation}
where $K_{\gamma}$ is a kernel on $\mathcal{S}$ with bandwidth $\gamma>0$, $\delta_{\mathbf{z}}$ denotes the point mass at $\mathbf{z}$, and the superscript $(-i)$ indicates that observation $i$ is left out.  FREM compares this kernel-weighted conditional distribution with the leave-one-out empirical marginal distribution $\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i)}_{\mathbf{Z}}=(n-1)^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{\mathbf{Z}_j}$, which yields the estimator
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\operatorname{EIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)
  :=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}
  \left(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i),\gamma}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=S_i},
        \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i)}_{\mathbf{Z}}\right).
  \label{eq:eipm-estimator}
\end{equation}
With a general discriminator class, this estimator requires a separate maximization at every $S_i$.  FREM avoids these maximizations by taking $\mathcal{V}$ to be the unit ball of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS), for which each IPM is the MMD and has a closed-form kernel expression \citep{gretton2012kernel,kong2025fair}.  For a supervised loss $\ell$ and a penalty parameter $\lambda\ge 0$, FREM then trains the encoder and the prediction head by minimizing
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \ell\!\left(f(\mathbf{Z}_i),Y_i\right)
  +\lambda\,\widehat{\operatorname{EIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S).
  \label{eq:frem-objective}
\end{equation}

\subsection{ReLU-IPM}
\label{sec:relu-ipm}

The RKHS unit ball of Section~\ref{sec:frem} determines the prediction heads that EIPM controls.  The RKHS of the Gaussian kernel, however, contains only infinitely differentiable functions \citep{park2025relu}, so a prediction head of lower smoothness lies outside $\mathcal{V}$.  \citet{park2025relu} address this restriction with ReLU-IPM, an IPM whose discriminator class is indexed by a finite-dimensional parameter and which nevertheless controls every prediction head of bounded H\"older norm.

Let $\mathbb{B}^d:=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^d:\lVert \mathbf{z}\rVert_2\le 1\}$ be the unit ball and $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^d:\lVert\theta\rVert_2=1\}$ the unit sphere.  \citet{park2025relu} define the ReLU discriminator class on $\mathbb{B}^d$ by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}
  :=\left\{
    v_{\theta,\mu}(\mathbf{z})=(\theta^\top \mathbf{z}+\mu)_+:
    \theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\ \mu\in[-1,1]
  \right\},
  \label{eq:relu-class}
\end{equation}
where $(a)_+:=\max\{a,0\}$.  The ReLU-IPM between probability distributions $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$ is $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})$, that is, Equation~(\ref{eq:prelim-ipm}) with $\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$.  Although $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$ is indexed by the finite-dimensional parameter $(\theta,\mu)$ alone, ReLU-IPM separates probability distributions on $\mathbb{B}^d$ \citep{park2025relu}.  The finite dimension of $(\theta,\mu)$ makes this supremum computable: \citet{park2025relu} evaluate ReLU-IPM between empirical distributions by gradient ascent over $(\theta,\mu)$, projecting $\theta$ back onto the unit sphere after each step.

To describe the prediction heads that ReLU-IPM controls, fix a smoothness index $\beta>0$ and let $k$ be the largest integer strictly smaller than $\beta$.  Let $\partial^{\mathbf{a}}$ denote the partial derivative indexed by a multi-index $\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}_0^d$ of order $|\mathbf{a}|=a_1+\cdots+a_d$.  Then the $\beta$-H\"older norm of a $k$ times continuously differentiable function $v:\mathbb{B}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ and the $\beta$-H\"older ball on $\mathbb{B}^d$ are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}}
  &:=\max\left\{
    \max_{|\mathbf{a}|\le k}\,\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{B}^d}
      \left|\partial^{\mathbf{a}}v(\mathbf{z})\right|,\;
    \max_{|\mathbf{a}|=k}\,\sup_{\mathbf{z}\ne\mathbf{z}'\in\mathbb{B}^d}
      \frac{\left|\partial^{\mathbf{a}}v(\mathbf{z})-\partial^{\mathbf{a}}v(\mathbf{z}')\right|}
           {\lVert\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}'\rVert_2^{\beta-k}}
  \right\},
  \\
  \mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d
  &:=\left\{v\in C^{k}(\mathbb{B}^d):\lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}}\le 1\right\},
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:holder-ball}
\end{equation}
and H\"older-IPM denotes $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}$.  \citet{park2025relu} establish that, for every $\beta>0$ with $\beta\ne(d+3)/2$, there exists a constant $C$ depending only on $d$ and $\beta$ such that, for all probability measures $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})
  \le C
  \begin{cases}
    \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})^{\frac{2\beta}{d+3}},
      & \beta<\frac{d+3}{2},\\[2mm]
    \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q}),
      & \beta>\frac{d+3}{2}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:relu-holder}
\end{equation}
A bound on ReLU-IPM therefore implies a bound on the H\"older-IPM.

\section{supIPM for fair representation learning}
\label{sec:supipm}

\subsection{From GDP to supIPM}
\label{sec:supipm-criterion}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{motivation.pdf}
  \caption{The conditional and the marginal distribution of the representation coincide at a typical sensitive value and differ at $s_0$, a sensitive value where few observations lie.}
  \label{fig:motivation}
\end{figure}

\citet{jiang2022gdp} define GDP for a continuous sensitive attribute as the average over $S$ of the absolute difference between the conditional and the marginal mean prediction.  For a bounded measurable function $g:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}$,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{GDP}(g)
  :=\mathbb{E}_{S}\!\left[
    \left|
      \mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S]
      -\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})]
    \right|
  \right].
  \label{eq:gdp}
\end{equation}

Because Equation~(\ref{eq:gdp}) averages this discrepancy over $S$, a large discrepancy confined to a region of small $\mathbb{P}_S$-probability can contribute little to $\operatorname{GDP}(g)$.  Figure~\ref{fig:motivation} illustrates such a case: the discrepancy between the conditional and the marginal distribution is large at the worst-case value $s_0$, whose neighborhood carries too little probability to affect the average over $S$.  Categorical DP, by contrast, imposes one fairness level on every group regardless of its probability.  An extension to a continuous sensitive attribute should meet the same standard at every sensitive value.  We therefore replace the average by a supremum and define $L_{\infty}$-GDP, written $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}$, as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}(g)
  :=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
  \left|
    \mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s]
    -\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})]
  \right|.
  \label{eq:linfty-gdp}
\end{equation}
The supremum in Equation~(\ref{eq:linfty-gdp}) requires the conditional expectation at every $s\in\mathcal{S}$.
% , not only for $\mathbb{P}_S$-almost every $s$.  
We impose the following condition on the distribution of $S$.
\begin{assumption}[Positive density]
\label{as:positive-density}
$S$ has a density $p_S$ with respect to the Lebesgue measure $\mathrm{Leb}$ on $\mathcal{S}$, and $0<c_S\le p_S(s)\le C_S<\infty$ for every $s\in\mathcal{S}$.
\end{assumption}
Assumption~\ref{as:positive-density} is adopted from FREM \citep{kong2025fair} and is maintained throughout.  Let $p(\mathbf{x},s)$ be a joint density of $(\mathbf{X},S)$ with respect to $\nu\otimes\mathrm{Leb}$ for a $\sigma$-finite measure $\nu$ on $\mathcal{X}$.  Fixing a version of $p$ with $\int_{\mathcal{X}} p(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x})=p_S(s)$ for every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, we obtain a version of the conditional expectation that is defined at every $s$, namely
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s]
  :=\frac{1}{p_S(s)}\int_{\mathcal{X}} g(\mathbf{x})\,p(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x}),
  \qquad s\in\mathcal{S},
  \label{eq:pointwise-conditional}
\end{equation}
and this version is used in what follows, in particular inside the supremum in Equation~(\ref{eq:linfty-gdp}).

The value $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}(f\circ h)$ can be evaluated only once the prediction head $f$ is fixed.  We therefore propose a criterion for the representation itself, which controls an entire class of prediction heads at once.  Such a criterion is the IPM of a discriminator class.

Because the discriminator classes of Section~\ref{sec:relu-ipm} are defined on $\mathbb{B}^d$, we map the representation $\mathbf{Z}=h_{\phi}(\mathbf{X})$ of the parameterized encoder $h_{\phi}$ in Section~\ref{sec:frem} into the unit ball by $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}:=\mathbf{Z}/\sqrt{\lVert\mathbf{Z}\rVert_2^2+1}\in\mathbb{B}^d$.  From here on, prediction heads and discriminator classes are defined on $\mathbb{B}^d$ and applied to $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}$.  The conditional distribution $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ is defined as in Equation~(\ref{eq:pointwise-conditional}).  For a discriminator class $\mathcal{V}$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$, we define
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}
  \left(\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}\right) \notag\\
  &=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
  \left|
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\mid S=s\right]
    -\mathbb{E}\!\left[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\right]
  \right|.
  \label{eq:supipm}
\end{align}
Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm}) is the representation-level counterpart of $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}$: it measures the largest discrepancy between the conditional and marginal distributions before a prediction head is chosen.

By Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm}), the constraint $\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)\le\rho$ for a fairness level $\rho\ge0$ bounds $\left|\mathbb{E}[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\mid S=s]-\mathbb{E}[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})]\right|$ by $\rho$ for every discriminator $v\in\mathcal{V}$ and every $s\in\mathcal{S}$.  For comparison with EIPM, write $\operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$ for Equation(~\ref{eq:eipm}) with $\mathbf{Z}$ replaced by $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}$.  Because an average never exceeds a supremum, $\operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)\le\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$, so a supIPM bound of $\rho$ implies an EIPM bound at the same fairness level.  The converse fails without further regularity in $s$.

It remains to choose the discriminator class $\mathcal{V}$.  Because $\mathcal{V}$ fixes the prediction heads that supIPM controls, a guarantee for every head of bounded H\"older norm calls for the H\"older ball $\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d$.  The H\"older-IPM cannot be computed directly, so we take $\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$, the ReLU discriminator class of Section~\ref{sec:relu-ipm}, for which the inner IPM in Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm}) is the ReLU-IPM.  The inequality in Equation~(\ref{eq:relu-holder}) applies to $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ and $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}$ at every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, so a bound on $\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$ implies a bound on $\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$.

\subsection{Estimation of supIPM}
\label{sec:supipm-estimation}

For the sample $\mathcal{D}$ of Section~\ref{sec:frem}, let $\mathbf{Z}_i=h_{\phi}(\mathbf{X}_i)$ and $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i=\mathbf{Z}_i/\sqrt{\lVert\mathbf{Z}_i\rVert_2^2+1}$.  Estimating supIPM requires the conditional distribution $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ at every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, whereas FREM requires it only at the observed values $S_i$.  We therefore adapt the kernel-weighted construction of Equation~(\ref{eq:frem-conditional}) to an arbitrary $s$.  Because $s$ is no longer tied to an observation, the leave-one-out correction is not needed, and all $n$ observations are used in both the conditional and the marginal estimates:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)
  :=\frac{K_{\gamma}(S_i,s)}{\sum_{j=1}^n K_{\gamma}(S_j,s)},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}
  :=\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)\,
  \delta_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}
  :=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i},
  \label{eq:supipm-estimators}
\end{equation}
where $K_{\gamma}$ is a kernel on $\mathcal{S}$ with bandwidth $\gamma>0$.  Observations whose sensitive values are close to $s$ receive larger weights.  The weights sum to one over $i$, so that $\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ is a probability distribution.  Substituting the two empirical distributions into Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm}) yields
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  (\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  \left(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s},
        \widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}\right) \notag\\
  &=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
    \sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\,\mu\in[-1,1]}
  \left|
    \sum_{i=1}^n
    \left(\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)-\frac{1}{n}\right)
    (\theta^\top\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i+\mu)_+
  \right|.
  \label{eq:empirical-supipm}
\end{align}
Whereas the EIPM estimator in Equation~(\ref{eq:eipm-estimator}) averages the discrepancy at the observed sensitive values, Equation~(\ref{eq:empirical-supipm}) takes the largest estimated discrepancy over all of $\mathcal{S}$.  When $K_{\gamma}$ is differentiable, the weights are differentiable in $s$, so gradient ascent over $\mathcal{S}$ can search for the largest discrepancy.  
% Section~\ref{sec:supipm-objective} performs this search during training.

\subsection{Learning with the supIPM penalty}
\label{sec:supipm-objective}
%s^* 찾는 알고리즘 수정 처음엔 0.3 0.5 0.7 중에 큰거 0.7이 뽑혔으면 이제 0.6 0.7 0.8 중에 큰거. 0.6이 뽑혔으면 0.55 0.6 0.65 중 큰거... 이런식으로 찾는 알고리즘으로 수정.
We use the empirical supIPM as a penalty and train the encoder $h_{\phi}$ and the prediction head $f_{\psi}$ jointly by minimizing
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\phi,\psi)
  :=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
  \ell\!\left(f_{\psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i),Y_i\right)
  +\lambda\,
  \widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  (\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S),
  \label{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is the supervised loss of Section~\ref{sec:frem} and $\lambda\ge0$ controls the accuracy--fairness trade-off.  Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}) is the Lagrangian relaxation of minimizing the empirical supervised risk subject to $\widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)\le\rho$ for a fairness level $\rho\ge0$, as in Section~\ref{sec:supipm-criterion}.

Minimizing Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}) requires evaluating the empirical supIPM in Equation~(\ref{eq:empirical-supipm}), which contains two suprema: an outer supremum over the sensitive value $s\in\mathcal{S}$ and an inner supremum over the discriminator parameter $(\theta,\mu)$.  For $s\in\mathcal{S}$ and $(\theta,\mu)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]$, let $\Delta(s;\theta,\mu)$ denote the discrepancy detected at $s$ by the discriminator $v_{\theta,\mu}$:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta(s;\theta,\mu)
  :=\left|
    \sum_{i=1}^n
    \left(\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)-\frac{1}{n}\right)
    (\theta^\top\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i+\mu)_+
  \right|.
  \label{eq:discriminator-discrepancy}
\end{equation}
Then $\widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\,\mu\in[-1,1]}\Delta(s;\theta,\mu)$, so the minimization of Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}) is a min--max problem, which we solve by alternating gradient descent in $(\phi,\psi)$ with projected gradient ascent in $s$ and in $(\theta,\mu)$.

Because gradient ascent can converge to a local maximum, we follow \citet{park2025relu} and perform the ascent from several initial values in parallel, maintaining $T$ candidate sensitive values $\mathbf{s}:=(s^{(1)},\ldots,s^{(T)})$ and $M$ discriminators $\mathbf{v}:=((\theta_1,\mu_1),\ldots,(\theta_M,\mu_M))$, where $(\theta_m,\mu_m)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]$ for $m\in[M]:=\{1,\ldots,M\}$.  Let $s^{\star}:=\argmax_{s\in\{s^{(1)},\ldots,s^{(T)}\}}\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s;\theta_m,\mu_m)$ be the candidate at which the largest discrepancy is detected.  The candidates and the discriminators are updated by gradient ascent on
\begin{equation}
  G(\mathbf{s}):=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s^{(t)};\theta_m,\mu_m),
  \qquad
  H(\mathbf{v}):=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Delta(s^{\star};\theta_m,\mu_m),
  \label{eq:stacked-objectives}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}$ and $s^{\star}$ are held fixed at their current values in $G$ and in $H$, respectively.  The $t$-th summand of $G$ depends on $\mathbf{s}$ only through $s^{(t)}$, and the $m$-th summand of $H$ depends on $\mathbf{v}$ only through $(\theta_m,\mu_m)$, so one ascent step on $G$ moves all $T$ candidates and one ascent step on $H$ moves all $M$ discriminators.  We write $\Pi_{A}$ for the projection onto a set $A$.  After each ascent step, $\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}$ returns each candidate to the sensitive domain, and $\Pi_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]}$ returns each $\theta_m$ to the unit sphere and each $\mu_m$ to $[-1,1]$.  The encoder and the prediction head are then updated by gradient descent on $\mathcal{L}(\phi,\psi)$ with the empirical supIPM replaced by $\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s^{\star};\theta_m,\mu_m)$, that is, with the outer supremum taken over the $T$ candidates and the inner supremum over the $M$ discriminators.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:supipm-frl} assembles these three updates into $R$ iterations, and we name the resulting procedure supIPM-FRL.  Each iteration performs $J_v$ discriminator updates, each preceded by $J_s$ ascent steps of freshly initialized candidates, and closes with one descent step.  The discriminator updates, the candidate ascent steps and the descent step use the step sizes $\eta_v$, $\eta_s$ and $\eta$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{supIPM-FRL}
\label{alg:supipm-frl}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE sample $\mathcal{D}$, supervised loss $\ell$, kernel $K_{\gamma}$ with bandwidth $\gamma$, penalty parameter $\lambda$, number of candidates $T$, number of discriminators $M$
\ENSURE encoder $h_{\phi}$ and prediction head $f_{\psi}$
\STATE Initialize $\phi$, $\psi$, and $\mathbf{v}$ with $(\theta_m,\mu_m)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]$ for $m\in[M]$
\FOR{$r=1,\ldots,R$}
  \FOR{$j=1,\ldots,J_v$}
    \STATE Initialize $\mathbf{s}$ at $T$ distinct points of $\mathcal{S}$ \label{alg:s-init}
    \FOR{$J_s$ steps}
      \STATE $\mathbf{s}\leftarrow\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}\!\left(\mathbf{s}+\eta_s\,\nabla_{\mathbf{s}}G(\mathbf{s})\right)$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE $s^{\star}\leftarrow\argmax_{s\in\{s^{(1)},\ldots,s^{(T)}\}}\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s;\theta_m,\mu_m)$ \label{alg:s-star}
    \STATE $\mathbf{v}\leftarrow\Pi_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]}\!\left(\mathbf{v}+\eta_v\,\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}H(\mathbf{v})\right)$
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE Recompute $s^{\star}$ by repeating lines~\ref{alg:s-init}--\ref{alg:s-star} with the updated $\mathbf{v}$
  \STATE $(\phi,\psi)\leftarrow(\phi,\psi)-\eta\,\nabla_{(\phi,\psi)}\big[\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\ell(f_{\psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i),Y_i)+\lambda\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s^{\star};\theta_m,\mu_m)\big]$ \label{alg:encoder-step}
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $h_{\phi}$, $f_{\psi}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Equations~(\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}) and (\ref{eq:discriminator-discrepancy}) are built from sample averages, so the procedure applies unchanged when these averages are computed on the current minibatch.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

We establish two properties of supIPM with the ReLU discriminator class.  A small population supIPM bounds $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}$ of every prediction head of bounded H\"older norm, and the empirical supIPM in Equation~(\ref{eq:empirical-supipm}) converges to the population supIPM uniformly over the encoder class, so that the fairness level attained on the sample transfers to the population.  Proofs are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{Assumptions}
\label{sec:theory-assumptions}

Write $\mathcal{S}=[a,b]$ and $\tau(\mathbf{z}):=\mathbf{z}/\sqrt{\lVert\mathbf{z}\rVert_2^2+1}\in\mathbb{B}^d$, and for an encoder $h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^d$ let $\widetilde{h}:=\tau\circ h$, so that $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}=\tau(h_{\phi}(\mathbf{X}))$ is the normalized representation of Section~\ref{sec:supipm-criterion}.  Conditional expectations given $S=s$ are the versions in Equation~(\ref{eq:pointwise-conditional}).  The weights $\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)$ in Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm-estimators}) are those of a local constant smoother.  Within distance $\gamma$ of $a$ or of $b$ such a smoother collects only part of the kernel mass, and its bias there is of first order in $\gamma$ rather than of second order.  We therefore state the finite-sample results on the interior region $\mathcal{S}_n:=[a+\epsilon_n,\,b-\epsilon_n]$, whose trimming width satisfies $0<\epsilon_n<(b-a)/2$ and $\epsilon_n\to0$, and we write
\begin{align}
  D_n(h)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}\ \sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
     \left|\mathbb{E}\!\left[v(\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X}))\mid S=s\right]
           -\mathbb{E}\!\left[v(\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X}))\right]\right|,
  \label{eq:trimmed-supipm}\\
  \widehat{D}_n(h)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}\ \sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
     \left|\sum_{i=1}^n
       \left(\widehat{w}_{\gamma_n}(i;s)-\frac{1}{n}\right)
       v(\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X}_i))\right|,
  \label{eq:trimmed-empirical-supipm}
\end{align}
which are Equations~(\ref{eq:supipm}) and (\ref{eq:empirical-supipm}) evaluated at the encoder $h$, with the supremum over the sensitive domain restricted to $\mathcal{S}_n$ and with the convention $0/0:=0$ in $\widehat{w}_{\gamma_n}(i;s)$.  Since $\epsilon_n\to0$, every $s$ in the interior of $\mathcal{S}$ belongs to $\mathcal{S}_n$ for all large $n$.  Assumption~\ref{as:positive-density} is maintained throughout, and $p(\mathbf{x},s)$ is the joint density of $(\mathbf{X},S)$ with respect to $\nu\otimes\mathrm{Leb}$ fixed in Section~\ref{sec:supipm-criterion}.

\begin{assumption}[Smooth joint density]
\label{as:smooth-density}
The sample $\mathcal{D}$ consists of independent copies of $(\mathbf{X},S,Y)$.  For $\nu$-almost every $\mathbf{x}$ the map $s\mapsto p(\mathbf{x},s)$ is twice continuously differentiable on $\mathcal{S}$, and there exist $M_1,M_2\in L_1(\nu)$ such that $\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\left|\partial_s^{j}p(\mathbf{x},s)\right|\le M_j(\mathbf{x})$ for $j\in\{1,2\}$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{as:smooth-density} is the counterpart of Assumption~3 of \citet{kong2025fair}, with integrable envelopes in place of pointwise bounds.  The envelopes permit differentiation under the integral sign in $s\mapsto\int_{\mathcal{X}}g(\mathbf{x})p(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x})$ uniformly over bounded $g$, which is the form in which the smoothness is used.  No smoothness in $\mathbf{x}$ is required.

\begin{assumption}[Kernel, bandwidth and trimming width]
\label{as:kernel}
$K_{\gamma}(s',s)=\gamma^{-1}K((s'-s)/\gamma)$ for a bounded, symmetric and Lipschitz function $K:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)$ with $\int K=1$ and $\int t^2K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty$.  Writing $T_K(r):=\int_{|t|>r}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$ for the tail mass of $K$, the bandwidth and the trimming width satisfy $\gamma_n\to0$, $0<\gamma_n\le\min\{\epsilon_n,1/2\}$ and $T_K(\epsilon_n/\gamma_n)\le\gamma_n^2$.
\end{assumption}

The factor $\gamma^{-1}$ cancels in the weights of Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm-estimators}) and normalizes the kernel for the proofs.  The condition on $T_K$ restricts the kernel mass outside $[s-\epsilon_n,s+\epsilon_n]$ to the order of the squared-bandwidth bias, so that every $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$ behaves as an interior point.  A kernel supported on $[-1,1]$, such as the Epanechnikov or the biweight kernel, satisfies it with $T_K(\epsilon_n/\gamma_n)=0$, and the Gaussian kernel satisfies it for every polynomially decaying bandwidth once $\epsilon_n\asymp1/\log n$.

\begin{assumption}[Encoder class]
\label{as:encoder}
$\mathcal{E}_n$ is a pointwise measurable class \citep[Section~2.3.3]{vaart1996weak} of measurable encoders $h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^d$ such that, with $\lVert h-h'\rVert_\infty:=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\lVert h(\mathbf{x})-h'(\mathbf{x})\rVert_2$, there exist $A_n\ge e$ and $\mathfrak{v}_n\ge1$ with
\begin{equation}
  N(\eta,\mathcal{E}_n,\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)
  \le\left(\frac{A_n}{\eta}\right)^{\mathfrak{v}_n},
  \qquad 0<\eta\le1 .
  \label{eq:encoder-entropy}
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

The same sup-norm entropy condition enters Theorem~5 of \citet{kong2025fair}.  When $\mathcal{X}$ is bounded, a ReLU network of depth $L$ and width $H$ with bounded parameters satisfies Equation~(\ref{eq:encoder-entropy}) with $\mathfrak{v}_n$ of the order of the number of parameters and $\log A_n$ of the order of $L\log H$ \citep{schmidthieber2020nonparametric}, and both quantities may grow with $n$.  For $0<\delta<1$ we set
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{n,\delta}
  :=(\mathfrak{v}_n+d)\log\frac{A_nn}{\gamma_n}+\log\frac{1}{\delta},
  \qquad
  r_{n,\delta}
  :=\gamma_n^2+\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{n,\delta}}{n\gamma_n}} .
  \label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Worst-case fairness and uniform convergence}
\label{sec:theory-results}

\begin{theorem}[Fairness of prediction heads]
\label{thm:transfer}
Let $d\ge3$ and let $h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^d$ be measurable.  Let $\beta>0$ with $\beta\ne(d+3)/2$, and set $\alpha_{d,\beta}:=2\beta/(d+3)$ if $\beta<(d+3)/2$ and $\alpha_{d,\beta}:=1$ if $\beta>(d+3)/2$.  Write $\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d(B):=\{f\in C^{k}(\mathbb{B}^d):\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}}\le B\}$ for the H\"older ball of radius $B>0$ in Equation~(\ref{eq:holder-ball}).  There exists a constant $C_{d,\beta}$, depending only on $d$ and $\beta$, such that for every $B>0$,
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{f\in\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d(B)}
  \operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}(f\circ\widetilde{h})
  \le C_{d,\beta}\,B\,
  \operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  \left(\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X});S\right)^{\alpha_{d,\beta}} .
  \label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
If $\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X});S)=0$, then $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s}=\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X})}$ for every $s\in\mathcal{S}$ and $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}(f\circ\widetilde{h})=0$ for every bounded measurable $f$.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} is the worst-case counterpart of Theorems~1 and 2 of \citet{kong2025fair}: the bound holds at each sensitive value separately rather than on average over $S$, and the increasing concave modulus that part~2 of their Theorem~2 assumes is here supplied explicitly by Equation~(\ref{eq:relu-holder}).  For a head smoother than $(d+3)/2$ the exponent $\alpha_{d,\beta}$ equals one, so a supIPM of $\rho$ bounds the worst-case discrepancy by $C_{d,\beta}B\rho$; for a rougher head the bound is $C_{d,\beta}B\rho^{2\beta/(d+3)}$.  Because Equation~(\ref{eq:transfer}) is obtained at each $s$ separately, it remains valid when the two suprema over $s$ are restricted to $\mathcal{S}_n$, with the worst-case discrepancy over $\mathcal{S}_n$ on the left and $D_n(h)$ on the right.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform convergence of the empirical supIPM]
\label{thm:uniform}
Let Assumptions~\ref{as:positive-density}--\ref{as:encoder} hold.  There exist constants $C,c>0$, depending only on $c_S$, $C_S$, $\lVert M_2\rVert_{L_1(\nu)}$ and $K$, such that whenever $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\le c\,n\gamma_n$, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{h\in\mathcal{E}_n}
  \left|\widehat{D}_n(h)-D_n(h)\right|
  \le C\,r_{n,\delta} .
  \label{eq:uniform}
\end{equation}
On the same event, $\mathcal{E}_n(\rho-Cr_{n,\delta})\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_n(\rho)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_n(\rho+Cr_{n,\delta})$ for every fairness level $\rho\ge Cr_{n,\delta}$, where $\mathcal{E}_n(t):=\{h\in\mathcal{E}_n:D_n(h)\le t\}$ and $\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_n(t):=\{h\in\mathcal{E}_n:\widehat{D}_n(h)\le t\}$.
\end{theorem}

The rate $r_{n,\delta}$ takes the form familiar from kernel smoothing, a squared-bandwidth bias together with a stochastic term of order $(n\gamma_n)^{-1/2}$ inflated by the entropy of the encoder class.  The representation dimension enters only through the $d+1$ parameters of the ReLU discriminator, so the estimate is free of the curse of dimensionality in $\mathcal{Z}$, as \citet{kong2025fair} also observe for EIPM.  For a single fixed encoder, Equation~(\ref{eq:uniform}) is the analogue of their Theorem~4 and the two inclusions are the analogue of their Theorem~5: an encoder that meets the empirical constraint at level $\rho$ meets the population constraint at level $\rho+Cr_{n,\delta}$.

\begin{corollary}[Fairness certificate for the learned encoder]
\label{cor:certificate}
Let the assumptions of Theorems~\ref{thm:transfer} and \ref{thm:uniform} hold, and let $\widehat{h}_n\in\mathcal{E}_n$ be an encoder that may depend on $\mathcal{D}$, such as the encoder returned by Algorithm~\ref{alg:supipm-frl}.  On the event of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform}, simultaneously for every $B>0$ and every $\beta>0$ with $\beta\ne(d+3)/2$,
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{f\in\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d(B)}\ \sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}
  \left|\mathbb{E}\!\left[f(\tau(\widehat{h}_n(\mathbf{X})))\mid S=s\right]
        -\mathbb{E}\!\left[f(\tau(\widehat{h}_n(\mathbf{X})))\right]\right|
  \le C_{d,\beta}\,B
  \left\{\widehat{D}_n(\widehat{h}_n)+Cr_{n,\delta}\right\}^{\alpha_{d,\beta}} .
  \label{eq:certificate}
\end{equation}
If in addition $A_n$ and $\mathfrak{v}_n$ are bounded, $\delta_n=n^{-c_1}$ for some $c_1>0$, $\gamma_n\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/5}$ and $\epsilon_n\asymp1/\log n$, then $r_{n,\delta_n}=O\{(\log n/n)^{2/5}\}$.
\end{corollary}

The left-hand side of Equation~(\ref{eq:certificate}) is a population quantity attached to the encoder returned by Algorithm~\ref{alg:supipm-frl}, and one evaluation of the empirical supIPM bounds it for every H\"older prediction head and every sensitive value in $\mathcal{S}_n$.  Trimming affects the rate rather than the consistency: when the two suprema run over all of $\mathcal{S}$, as in Equations~(\ref{eq:supipm}) and (\ref{eq:empirical-supipm}), the partial kernel mass near $a$ and $b$ raises the bias there from $\gamma_n^2$ to $\gamma_n$, so that balancing the two terms of $r_{n,\delta}$ at $\gamma_n\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/3}$ gives uniform consistency over the entire sensitive domain at the rate $O\{(\log n/n)^{1/3}\}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:untrimmed}).

\section{Proofs for Section~\ref{sec:theory}}
\label{app:proofs}

Throughout the appendix $\mathbb{E}$ denotes expectation under the distribution of $(\mathbf{X},S)$ and $\mathbb{E}_n$ the average over the sample, $\mathbb{E}_n\varphi:=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\varphi(\mathbf{X}_i,S_i)$.  We abbreviate $\gamma:=\gamma_n$ and write $C$ for a constant that depends only on $c_S$, $C_S$, $\lVert M_1\rVert_{L_1(\nu)}$, $\lVert M_2\rVert_{L_1(\nu)}$ and $K$, whose value may change between occurrences.  The constant may also depend on the length of $\mathcal{S}$, which this list already controls: $p_S$ integrates to one and is bounded below by $c_S$, so $b-a\le1/c_S$.  The class of scores induced by the encoder class and the ReLU discriminators is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{G}_n:=\left\{\mathbf{x}\mapsto v(\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{x})):
    h\in\mathcal{E}_n,\ v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}\right\},
  \label{eq:score-class}
\end{equation}
and $0\le g\le2$ for every $g\in\mathcal{G}_n$, because $|\theta^\top\mathbf{z}+\mu|\le2$ whenever $\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{B}^d$, $\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ and $\mu\in[-1,1]$.  For $g\in\mathcal{G}_n$ and $s\in\mathcal{S}$ put
\begin{align*}
  a_g(s)&:=\int_{\mathcal{X}}g(\mathbf{x})p(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x}),
  & m_g(s)&:=\frac{a_g(s)}{p_S(s)}=\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s],\\
  b_{g,\gamma}(s)&:=\mathbb{E}\{K_{\gamma}(S,s)g(\mathbf{X})\},
  & q_{\gamma}(s)&:=\mathbb{E}K_{\gamma}(S,s),\\
  m_{g,\gamma}(s)&:=\frac{b_{g,\gamma}(s)}{q_{\gamma}(s)},
  & \widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)
  &:=\frac{\mathbb{E}_n\{K_{\gamma}(S,s)g(\mathbf{X})\}}{\mathbb{E}_nK_{\gamma}(S,s)}
   =\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)g(\mathbf{X}_i),
\end{align*}
where $m_g$ is the version of the conditional expectation fixed in Equation~(\ref{eq:pointwise-conditional}).  With $g=v\circ\widetilde{h}$ and the suprema taken over $v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$ and $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$, Equations~(\ref{eq:trimmed-supipm}) and (\ref{eq:trimmed-empirical-supipm}) read
\begin{equation}
  D_n(h)=\sup_{g,s}\left|m_g(s)-\mathbb{E}g\right|,
  \qquad
  \widehat{D}_n(h)=\sup_{g,s}\left|\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)-\mathbb{E}_ng\right| ,
  \label{eq:D-in-scores}
\end{equation}
and both are at most $2$.  We use Bernstein's inequality in the following form \citep[e.g.,][]{boucheron2013concentration}: if $W_1,\ldots,W_n$ are independent copies of a random variable $W$ with $0\le W\le M$ and $\operatorname{Var}(W)\le\sigma^2$, then
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^nW_i-\mathbb{E}W\right|\ge u\right)
  \le2\exp\left(-\frac{nu^2}{2\sigma^2+2Mu/3}\right),
  \qquad u>0 .
  \label{eq:bernstein}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}}
\label{app:proof-transfer}

\begin{proof}
Fix $s\in\mathcal{S}$ and write $\mathbb{P}_s:=\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s}$ and $\mathbb{P}_0:=\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X})}$, both probability measures on $\mathbb{B}^d$.  Let $f\in\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d(B)$, so that $f/B\in\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d$.  Equation~(\ref{eq:relu-holder}) \citep[Theorem~2]{park2025relu} gives
\[
  \left|\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_s-\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_0\right|
  \le B\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}(\mathbb{P}_s,\mathbb{P}_0)
  \le C_{d,\beta}B\,
     \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P}_s,\mathbb{P}_0)^{\alpha_{d,\beta}} .
\]
By Equation~(\ref{eq:supipm}) we have $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P}_s,\mathbb{P}_0)\le\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X});S)$, and $t\mapsto t^{\alpha_{d,\beta}}$ is increasing on $[0,\infty)$, so the right-hand side is at most $C_{d,\beta}B\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X});S)^{\alpha_{d,\beta}}$.  This bound does not depend on $s$, so taking the supremum over $s\in\mathcal{S}$ and then over $f$ proves Equation~(\ref{eq:transfer}); taking the supremum over $s$ in a subset of $\mathcal{S}$ instead proves the restricted statement.  Finally, ReLU-IPM separates probability distributions on $\mathbb{B}^d$ \citep[Proposition~1]{park2025relu}, so $\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{h}(\mathbf{X});S)=0$ forces $\mathbb{P}_s=\mathbb{P}_0$ for every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, and then $\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_s=\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_0$ for every bounded measurable $f$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform}}
\label{app:proof-uniform}

Both $\widehat{D}_n(h)$ and $D_n(h)$ lie in $[0,2]$, so Equation~(\ref{eq:uniform}) is trivial for $C\ge2$ once $r_{n,\delta}\ge1$.  We may therefore assume $r_{n,\delta}<1$.  Since $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\ge1$ and $\gamma\le1$, the condition $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\le cn\gamma$ also gives $\gamma\ge1/(cn)$.

\paragraph{Step 1: entropy of the score class.}
The map $\tau$ is $1$-Lipschitz on $\mathbb{R}^d$: its Jacobian at $\mathbf{z}$ is $u^{-1}(I_d-\mathbf{z}\mathbf{z}^\top/u^2)$ with $u:=(1+\lVert\mathbf{z}\rVert_2^2)^{1/2}$, a symmetric matrix whose eigenvalues are $u^{-1}$, with multiplicity $d-1$, and $u^{-3}$, all of them in $(0,1]$.  For $\mathbf{z},\mathbf{z}'\in\mathbb{B}^d$, $\theta,\theta'\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ and $\mu,\mu'\in[-1,1]$, the map $t\mapsto(t)_+$ is $1$-Lipschitz, so
\[
  \left|v_{\theta,\mu}(\mathbf{z})-v_{\theta',\mu'}(\mathbf{z}')\right|
  \le\left|\theta^\top(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}')\right|
    +\left|(\theta-\theta')^\top\mathbf{z}'\right|+|\mu-\mu'|
  \le\lVert\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}'\rVert_2+\lVert\theta-\theta'\rVert_2+|\mu-\mu'| .
\]
Hence $\lVert v_{\theta,\mu}\circ\widetilde{h}-v_{\theta',\mu'}\circ\widetilde{h}'\rVert_\infty\le\lVert h-h'\rVert_\infty+\lVert\theta-\theta'\rVert_2+|\mu-\mu'|$.  Covering $\mathcal{E}_n$ at scale $\eta$ by Assumption~\ref{as:encoder}, covering $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ and $[-1,1]$ at scale $\eta/2$, and using $A_n\ge e$ and $\mathfrak{v}_n\ge1$, we obtain an absolute constant $C_0\ge e$ with
\begin{equation}
  N(2\eta,\mathcal{G}_n,\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty)
  \le\left(\frac{A_n}{\eta}\right)^{\mathfrak{v}_n}
     \left(\frac{C_0}{\eta}\right)^{d+1}
  \le\left(\frac{A_n\vee C_0}{\eta}\right)^{2(\mathfrak{v}_n+d)},
  \qquad 0<\eta\le1 .
  \label{eq:score-entropy}
\end{equation}
Since $\mathcal{G}_n$ has sup-norm diameter at most $2$, a single centre suffices at any scale larger than $2$.

\paragraph{Step 2: uniform second-order bias.}
Let $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$ and put $r_n:=\epsilon_n/\gamma\ge1$ and $I_n:=[-r_n,r_n]$, so that $s+\gamma t\in\mathcal{S}$ for every $t\in I_n$.  Extend $p(\mathbf{x},\cdot)$ and $p_S$ by zero outside $\mathcal{S}$.  The substitution $s'=s+\gamma t$ gives
\begin{equation}
  b_{g,\gamma}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(t)\,a_g(s+\gamma t)\,\mathrm{d}t,
  \qquad
  q_{\gamma}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(t)\,p_S(s+\gamma t)\,\mathrm{d}t .
  \label{eq:change-of-variable}
\end{equation}
Since $0\le g\le2$ we have $0\le a_g\le2p_S\le2C_S$ on $\mathcal{S}$, so the contribution of $t\notin I_n$ to the first integral of Equation~(\ref{eq:change-of-variable}) is at most $2C_ST_K(r_n)\le2C_S\gamma^2$ by Assumption~\ref{as:kernel}; the corresponding contribution to $q_{\gamma}(s)$ is at most $C_S\gamma^2$.  Moreover
\[
  q_{\gamma}(s)\ge\int_{I_n}K(t)p_S(s+\gamma t)\,\mathrm{d}t
  \ge c_S\{1-T_K(r_n)\}\ge c_S(1-\gamma^2)\ge\tfrac{3}{4}c_S,
\]
because $\gamma\le1/2$, and $q_{\gamma}(s)\le C_S\int K=C_S$.  Under Assumption~\ref{as:smooth-density} the envelopes $M_1$ and $M_2$ permit differentiation under the integral sign, so $a_g$ and $p_S$ are twice continuously differentiable on $\mathcal{S}$ with
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_n}\lVert a_g''\rVert_\infty\le2\lVert M_2\rVert_{L_1(\nu)},
  \qquad
  \lVert p_S''\rVert_\infty\le\lVert M_2\rVert_{L_1(\nu)} .
  \label{eq:second-derivative}
\end{equation}
Taylor's theorem with the Lagrange remainder gives $|a_g(s+\gamma t)-a_g(s)-\gamma t\,a_g'(s)|\le\gamma^2t^2\lVert a_g''\rVert_\infty/2$ for every $t\in I_n$, while $\int_{I_n}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=1-T_K(r_n)$ and $\int_{I_n}tK(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=0$ because $K$ and $I_n$ are symmetric about the origin.  Hence
\[
  \left|\int_{I_n}K(t)\,a_g(s+\gamma t)\,\mathrm{d}t-a_g(s)\right|
  \le a_g(s)\,T_K(r_n)
    +\frac{\gamma^2}{2}\lVert a_g''\rVert_\infty\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^2K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t .
\]
Combining this with the tail bound above and with Equation~(\ref{eq:second-derivative}), and repeating the argument for $p_S$, we obtain, uniformly over $g\in\mathcal{G}_n$ and $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$,
\begin{equation}
  \left|b_{g,\gamma}(s)-a_g(s)\right|\le C\gamma^2,
  \qquad
  \left|q_{\gamma}(s)-p_S(s)\right|\le C\gamma^2 .
  \label{eq:bias}
\end{equation}
Writing $m_{g,\gamma}-m_g=(b_{g,\gamma}-a_g)/q_{\gamma}+m_g(p_S-q_{\gamma})/q_{\gamma}$ and using $0\le m_g\le2$ together with $q_{\gamma}\ge3c_S/4$,
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_n}\ \sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}
  \left|m_{g,\gamma}(s)-m_g(s)\right|\le C\gamma^2 .
  \label{eq:ratio-bias}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 3: uniform stochastic error.}
Fix $u$ with $n^{-1}\le u\le1$.  Let $g_1,\ldots,g_{N_1}$ be the centres of a $\zeta_g$-cover of $\mathcal{G}_n$ in $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\infty$ and let $s_1,\ldots,s_{N_2}$ be the centres of a $\zeta_s$-cover of $\mathcal{S}_n$ in $|\cdot|$, where
\[
  \zeta_g:=\frac{\gamma u}{8\lVert K\rVert_\infty},
  \qquad
  \zeta_s:=\frac{\gamma^2u}{16L_K}
\]
and $L_K$ is the Lipschitz constant of $K$.  Given $g\in\mathcal{G}_n$ and $s\in\mathcal{S}_n$, choose $k$ and $j$ with $\lVert g-g_k\rVert_\infty\le\zeta_g$ and $|s-s_j|\le\zeta_s$.  From $|K_{\gamma}(s',s)-K_{\gamma}(s',s_j)|\le L_K\gamma^{-2}|s-s_j|$, from $K_{\gamma}\le\lVert K\rVert_\infty/\gamma$ and from $0\le g_k\le2$,
\[
  \sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X},\,s'\in\mathcal{S}}
  \left|K_{\gamma}(s',s)g(\mathbf{x})-K_{\gamma}(s',s_j)g_k(\mathbf{x})\right|
  \le\frac{\lVert K\rVert_\infty}{\gamma}\zeta_g+\frac{2L_K}{\gamma^2}\zeta_s
  \le\frac{u}{4},
\]
whence $\left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})\{K_{\gamma}(S,s)g(\mathbf{X})\}\right|\le\left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})\{K_{\gamma}(S,s_j)g_k(\mathbf{X})\}\right|+u/2$.  The variable $W:=K_{\gamma}(S,s_j)g_k(\mathbf{X})$ obeys $0\le W\le2\lVert K\rVert_\infty/\gamma=:M$ and, by $q_{\gamma}\le C_S$,
\[
  \operatorname{Var}(W)\le\mathbb{E}W^2
  \le\frac{4\lVert K\rVert_\infty}{\gamma}\,\mathbb{E}K_{\gamma}(S,s_j)
  \le\frac{4\lVert K\rVert_\infty C_S}{\gamma}=:\sigma^2 .
\]
Applying Equation~(\ref{eq:bernstein}) at $u/2$ to each of the $N_1N_2$ pairs and taking a union bound,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{P}\left(
    \sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_n,\,s\in\mathcal{S}_n}
    \left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})\{K_{\gamma}(S,s)g(\mathbf{X})\}\right|>u
  \right)
  \le2N_1N_2\exp\left(-\frac{nu^2}{8\sigma^2+4Mu/3}\right).
  \label{eq:union}
\end{equation}
By Equation~(\ref{eq:score-entropy}) at $\eta=\zeta_g/2$, by $N_2\le1+(b-a)/(2\zeta_s)$, and by $u\ge n^{-1}$, $\gamma\ge1/(cn)$ and $A_n\ge e$,
\begin{equation}
  \log(N_1N_2)
  \le C\left\{(\mathfrak{v}_n+d)\log\frac{A_n}{\gamma u}
    +\log\frac{1}{\gamma^2u}\right\}
  \le C(\mathfrak{v}_n+d)\log\frac{A_nn}{\gamma} .
  \label{eq:log-cover}
\end{equation}
Since $\sigma^2$ and $M$ are of order $\gamma^{-1}$ and $u\le1$, the right-hand side of Equation~(\ref{eq:union}) is at most $\delta/3$ as soon as
\[
  u\ \ge\ C\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{n,\delta}}{n\gamma}}
    +\frac{\Gamma_{n,\delta}}{n\gamma}\right\},
\]
and here the second term is dominated by the first because $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\le cn\gamma$.  Taking $u$ equal to the resulting multiple of $\sqrt{\Gamma_{n,\delta}/(n\gamma)}$, which lies in $[n^{-1},1]$ because $\Gamma_{n,\delta}\ge1$, $\gamma\le1$ and $r_{n,\delta}<1$, we obtain with probability at least $1-\delta/3$
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_n,\,s\in\mathcal{S}_n}
  \left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})\{K_{\gamma}(S,s)g(\mathbf{X})\}\right|
  \le C\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{n,\delta}}{n\gamma}} .
  \label{eq:num-stoch}
\end{equation}
The same argument applied to the class $\{s'\mapsto K_{\gamma}(s',s):s\in\mathcal{S}_n\}$, for which only the cover of $\mathcal{S}_n$ is needed, and to the class $\mathcal{G}_n$, whose members satisfy $M=2$ and $\operatorname{Var}\le4$ and which is covered at scale $u/4$, gives on an event of probability at least $1-\delta$ containing the event of Equation~(\ref{eq:num-stoch}),
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}\left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})K_{\gamma}(S,s)\right|
  \le C\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{n,\delta}}{n\gamma}},
  \qquad
  \sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_n}\left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})g\right|
  \le C\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{n,\delta}}{n\gamma}},
  \label{eq:den-marg-stoch}
\end{equation}
the second bound using $\gamma\le1$.  Choosing $c$ small enough that the constant in Equation~(\ref{eq:den-marg-stoch}) satisfies $C\sqrt{c}\le c_S/4$, the lower bound $q_{\gamma}\ge3c_S/4$ of Step~2 gives, on the same event,
\begin{equation}
  \inf_{s\in\mathcal{S}_n}\mathbb{E}_nK_{\gamma}(S,s)\ \ge\ \frac{c_S}{2}>0,
  \label{eq:den-lower}
\end{equation}
so that the convention $0/0:=0$ in Equation~(\ref{eq:trimmed-empirical-supipm}) is never invoked.  Writing $A_{g,s}:=(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})\{K_{\gamma}(S,s)g(\mathbf{X})\}$ and $B_s:=(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})K_{\gamma}(S,s)$, the identity
\[
  \widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)-m_{g,\gamma}(s)
  =\frac{A_{g,s}-m_{g,\gamma}(s)B_s}{\mathbb{E}_nK_{\gamma}(S,s)},
  \qquad 0\le m_{g,\gamma}(s)\le2,
\]
combined with Equations~(\ref{eq:num-stoch})--(\ref{eq:den-lower}) and with the bias bound in Equation~(\ref{eq:ratio-bias}), yields
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}_n,\,s\in\mathcal{S}_n}
  \left|\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)-m_g(s)\right|
  \le C\left\{\gamma^2+\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{n,\delta}}{n\gamma}}\right\}
  =C\,r_{n,\delta} .
  \label{eq:cond-mean-uniform}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 4: conclusion.}
For real-valued functions $F$ and $G$ on a common index set, $\left|\sup_t|F(t)|-\sup_t|G(t)|\right|\le\sup_t|F(t)-G(t)|$.  Applied to Equation~(\ref{eq:D-in-scores}), this gives, for every $h\in\mathcal{E}_n$,
\[
  \left|\widehat{D}_n(h)-D_n(h)\right|
  \le\sup_{g,s}\left|\widehat{m}_{g,\gamma}(s)-m_g(s)\right|
    +\sup_{g}\left|(\mathbb{E}_n-\mathbb{E})g\right|
  \le C\,r_{n,\delta}
\]
by Equations~(\ref{eq:cond-mean-uniform}) and (\ref{eq:den-marg-stoch}).  The right-hand side does not depend on $h$, which proves Equation~(\ref{eq:uniform}).  If $h\in\mathcal{E}_n(\rho-Cr_{n,\delta})$, then $\widehat{D}_n(h)\le D_n(h)+Cr_{n,\delta}\le\rho$ on this event, and if $\widehat{D}_n(h)\le\rho$, then $D_n(h)\le\rho+Cr_{n,\delta}$.  These are the two inclusions stated in Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform}.
\qed

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:certificate}}
\label{app:proof-certificate}

\begin{proof}
Equation~(\ref{eq:uniform}) holds uniformly over $\mathcal{E}_n$, so it applies to $\widehat{h}_n$ even though $\widehat{h}_n$ depends on $\mathcal{D}$, and on the event of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform} we have $D_n(\widehat{h}_n)\le\widehat{D}_n(\widehat{h}_n)+Cr_{n,\delta}$.  By the restricted form of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}, the left-hand side of Equation~(\ref{eq:certificate}) is at most $C_{d,\beta}BD_n(\widehat{h}_n)^{\alpha_{d,\beta}}$, and $t\mapsto t^{\alpha_{d,\beta}}$ is increasing, so Equation~(\ref{eq:certificate}) follows.  The event of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform} depends on neither $\beta$ nor $B$, so the bound holds simultaneously for all of them.

For the rate, bounded $A_n$ and $\mathfrak{v}_n$ together with $\delta_n=n^{-c_1}$ give $\Gamma_{n,\delta_n}\asymp\log n$, and with $\gamma_n\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/5}$ both terms of $r_{n,\delta_n}$ are of order $(\log n/n)^{2/5}$.  The requirement $\Gamma_{n,\delta_n}\le cn\gamma_n$ reads $\log n=O\{n^{4/5}(\log n)^{1/5}\}$ and the requirement $\gamma_n\le\epsilon_n$ reads $(\log n/n)^{1/5}=O(1/\log n)$, and both hold for all large $n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Estimation without trimming}
\label{app:untrimmed}

Let the suprema in Equations~(\ref{eq:trimmed-supipm}) and (\ref{eq:trimmed-empirical-supipm}) run over all of $\mathcal{S}$, so that they reduce to Equations~(\ref{eq:supipm}) and (\ref{eq:empirical-supipm}).  Assumptions~\ref{as:positive-density}, \ref{as:smooth-density} and \ref{as:encoder} remain in force, and Assumption~\ref{as:kernel} is retained with its two trimming conditions $\gamma_n\le\epsilon_n$ and $T_K(\epsilon_n/\gamma_n)\le\gamma_n^2$ omitted, so that the bandwidth satisfies $\gamma_n\to0$ and $0<\gamma_n\le1/2$ alone.  Only Step~2 of Appendix~\ref{app:proof-uniform} changes; Steps~1, 3 and 4 use Assumption~\ref{as:kernel} only through the regularity of $K$ and through $\gamma\le1$.  Fix $s\in\mathcal{S}$, put $T_s:=\{t\in\mathbb{R}:s+\gamma t\in\mathcal{S}\}$ and $\kappa(s):=\int_{T_s}K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$.  The interval $T_s$ contains $[0,(b-a)/(2\gamma)]$ or $[-(b-a)/(2\gamma),0]$, because $s$ is at distance at least $(b-a)/2$ from one of the two endpoints of $\mathcal{S}$, so the symmetry of $K$ gives
\[
  \kappa(s)\ \ge\ \tfrac{1}{2}-T_K\!\left(\tfrac{b-a}{2\gamma}\right)\ \ge\ \tfrac{1}{4}
\]
for every $n$ large enough that $T_K\{(b-a)/(2\gamma_n)\}\le1/4$, and as soon as $\gamma_n\le(b-a)/2$ when $K$ is supported on $[-1,1]$.  A first-order Taylor expansion on $T_s$, together with $\lVert a_g'\rVert_\infty\le2\lVert M_1\rVert_{L_1(\nu)}$ and $\lVert p_S'\rVert_\infty\le\lVert M_1\rVert_{L_1(\nu)}$, gives
\[
  b_{g,\gamma}(s)=\kappa(s)a_g(s)+R_b,
  \qquad
  q_{\gamma}(s)=\kappa(s)p_S(s)+R_q,
\]
with $|R_b|\vee|R_q|\le2\lVert M_1\rVert_{L_1(\nu)}\,\gamma\int|t|K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$,
where $\int|t|K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty$ by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and Assumption~\ref{as:kernel}.  Since $q_{\gamma}(s)\ge c_S\kappa(s)\ge c_S/4$ and $a_g(s)/p_S(s)\le2$,
\[
  \left|m_{g,\gamma}(s)-m_g(s)\right|
  =\frac{\left|R_bp_S(s)-a_g(s)R_q\right|}{p_S(s)q_{\gamma}(s)}
  \le\frac{|R_b|+2|R_q|}{q_{\gamma}(s)}
  \le\frac{4(|R_b|+2|R_q|)}{c_S}
  \le C\gamma,
\]
uniformly over $g\in\mathcal{G}_n$ and $s\in\mathcal{S}$.  Steps~1, 3 and 4 of Appendix~\ref{app:proof-uniform} go through with $\mathcal{S}_n$ replaced by $\mathcal{S}$, with $c$ re-chosen so that $C\sqrt{c}\le c_S/8$, and with $c_S/2$ replaced by $c_S/8$ in Equation~(\ref{eq:den-lower}).  The untrimmed estimator therefore satisfies Equation~(\ref{eq:uniform}) with $\gamma_n^2$ replaced by $\gamma_n$ in $r_{n,\delta}$.  If $A_n$ and $\mathfrak{v}_n$ are bounded and $\delta_n=n^{-c_1}$ for some $c_1>0$, balancing the two terms at $\gamma_n\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/3}$ gives the rate $O\{(\log n/n)^{1/3}\}$.
\qed

\subsection{Margins in LaTeX}

Most of the margin problems come from figures positioned by hand using
\verb+\special+ or other commands. We suggest using the command
\verb+\includegraphics+
from the graphicx package. Always specify the figure width as a multiple of
the line width as in the example below using .eps graphics
\begin{verbatim}
   \usepackage[dvips]{graphicx} ...
   \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{myfile.eps}
\end{verbatim}
or % Apr 2009 addition
\begin{verbatim}
   \usepackage[pdftex]{graphicx} ...
   \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{myfile.pdf}
\end{verbatim}
for .pdf graphics.
See section~4.4 in the graphics bundle documentation (\url{http://www.ctan.org/tex-archive/macros/latex/required/graphics/grfguide.ps})

A number of width problems arise when LaTeX cannot properly hyphenate a
line. Please give LaTeX hyphenation hints using the \verb+\-+ command.
