\begin{abstract}
Fair representation learning (FRL) seeks a representation that is informative for downstream prediction yet independent of a sensitive attribute. For a continuous sensitive attribute, existing methods measure the discrepancy between conditional and marginal distributions at each sensitive value and \emph{average} it over the distribution of the sensitive attribute. Such an average can be small even when a large discrepancy is confined to a low-probability region, leaving some subpopulations unprotected. We propose $L_\infty$-generalized demographic parity, which replaces the average by a supremum, and its representation-level counterpart, the supremum integral probability metric (supIPM). We then propose supIPM-FRL, which learns a representation under a constraint on the empirical supIPM. The procedure estimates conditional distributions by kernel weighting and measures their discrepancy from the marginal distribution by ReLU-IPM. 
% Such a discriminator is determined by a direction and a bias alone, so each discrepancy is maximized over the discriminator class by gradient ascent. 
We prove that the empirical supIPM is a uniformly consistent estimator, so the fairness level attained on the sample transfers to the population. 
% The transfer is uniform over the sensitive domain and over prediction heads of bounded H\"older norm. 
Experiments on tabular and graph benchmarks show that, at every fairness level, supIPM-FRL attains prediction performance comparable to existing methods while substantially reducing the worst-case discrepancy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A learned representation serves many downstream tasks, including tasks that are unspecified when the representation is trained \citep{bengio2013representation}.  This transferability makes the fairness requirement more demanding, because constraining one prediction head does not prevent another from exploiting sensitive information retained in the representation \citep{madras2018adversarially}.  Fair representation learning (FRL) addresses this problem by seeking a representation that is informative for prediction and independent of the sensitive attribute $S$ \citep{zemel2013learning}.
%, so that every measurable function of the representation is again independent of $S$


Most existing FRL methods have been developed for a categorical $S$.  In this case, demographic parity (DP) requires the prediction to have the same distribution in every group, and these methods enforce DP by matching the distribution of the representation across groups.  One line of work trains an adversary to recover group membership from the representation \citep{edwards2016censoring,madras2018adversarially}.  Another compares the distribution of the representation across groups through the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) of a fixed kernel \citep{louizos2016variational}, an integral probability metric (IPM) over a prespecified parametric discriminator class \citep{kim2022parametric}, or the power of an MMD two-sample test with a learned kernel \citep{deka2023mmdbfair}.  This groupwise construction does not extend directly to a continuous $S$ such as age or income.  Distinct observations take distinct sensitive values with probability one, so at most one observation is available at any given sensitive value $s$, and one observation does not suffice to estimate the conditional distribution of the representation at $S=s$.  Binning $S$ restores the groupwise construction, but the resulting criterion depends on the choice of cutpoints \citep{kong2025fair}.  The discretization also introduces threshold effects and discards the ordering of the sensitive values \citep{mary2019continuous}.

Existing methods that treat $S$ as continuous have largely focused on the fitted predictor rather than on the representation, measuring its dependence on $S$ through maximal correlation or through generalized demographic parity (GDP) \citep{mary2019continuous,grari2020continuous,jiang2022gdp}.  Of these, GDP is the average over $S$ of the absolute difference between the conditional and marginal mean predictions.  \citet{kong2025fair} apply the same average at the representation level: the expectation of integral probability metrics (EIPM) is the average over $S$ of the IPM between the conditional distribution of the representation given $S=s$ and its marginal distribution.  Both criteria average the discrepancy over the distribution of $S$, so a large discrepancy confined to a low-probability range of sensitive values contributes little to the average.  The average thus departs from categorical DP, which imposes one fairness level on every group regardless of its probability.

A new criterion for a continuous $S$ should therefore hold at every sensitive value.  For a representation, the criterion should also hold for every prediction head that may later be fitted on it.  In this paper we propose criteria that take the supremum over $S$ in place of the average, so that a single bound holds uniformly over the sensitive domain.  This replacement transforms GDP into $L_{\infty}$-generalized demographic parity ($L_{\infty}$-GDP) and EIPM into the supremum integral probability metric (supIPM).  Our estimator of supIPM obtains the conditional distribution at an arbitrary $s$ from kernel weights, without binning, and searches the sensitive domain for the largest discrepancy from the marginal distribution.  The discrepancy is measured by the ReLU-IPM of \citet{park2025relu}, whose discriminators are single rectified linear units (ReLU) and which controls every prediction head of bounded H\"older norm.  We add this supIPM estimate as a penalty to the supervised risk and train the encoder and the prediction head with minibatches.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We introduce $L_{\infty}$-GDP, a fairness criterion for prediction models, and supIPM, its representation-level counterpart.  Both extend the groupwise requirement of categorical DP to a continuous sensitive attribute by measuring the worst-case discrepancy over the sensitive domain.
  \item We estimate supIPM from kernel weights and ReLU-IPM, and propose supIPM-FRL, an adversarial procedure that is compatible with minibatch training.
  \item We prove that the empirical supIPM is a uniformly consistent estimator, so that the fairness level attained on the sample transfers to the population.
  % uniformly over the sensitive domain and over prediction heads of bounded H\"older norm.
\end{itemize}

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

\subsection{Fair representation using EIPM (FREM)}
\label{sec:frem}

We observe an input $\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{X}$, an outcome $Y\in\mathcal{Y}$, and a continuous sensitive attribute $S$ taking values in the sensitive domain $\mathcal{S}$, a compact interval of $\mathbb{R}$.  An encoder $h:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}$ maps $\mathbf{X}$ to a representation $\mathbf{Z}=h(\mathbf{X})$ in the representation space $\mathcal{Z}=\mathbb{R}^d$, where $d$ is the representation dimension.  A prediction head $f:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}$ maps the representation to a real-valued prediction, and $f\circ h$ denotes the composed prediction model.  When the encoder and the prediction head are parameterized, we write them as $h_{\phi}$ and $f_{\psi}$ with parameters $\phi$ and $\psi$.  We write $\mathbb{P}_S$ for the distribution of $S$, $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ for the marginal distribution of $\mathbf{Z}$, and $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}$ for the conditional distribution of $\mathbf{Z}$ given $S=s$.  
% Equality $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ for $\mathbb{P}_S$-almost every $s$ is equivalent to independence of $\mathbf{Z}$ and $S$, and this independence is inherited by every measurable function of $\mathbf{Z}$.

The discrepancy between $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}$ and $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ is measured through a discriminator class $\mathcal{V}$, a class of real-valued discriminator functions on $\mathcal{Z}$. The IPM associated with $\mathcal{V}$ between two probability measures $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})
  :=\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
  \left|\int v(\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{z})-\int v(\mathbf{z})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{z})\right|.
  \label{eq:prelim-ipm}
\end{equation}
We say that $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}$ separates probability distributions if $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})=0$ implies $\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{Q}$.  The class $\mathcal{V}$ determines which prediction heads the IPM controls, because $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})$ bounds $\left|\int v\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}-\int v\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}\right|$ for every $v\in\mathcal{V}$, including any prediction head in $\mathcal{V}$.

For a continuous sensitive attribute, \citet{kong2025fair} propose fair representation using EIPM (FREM).  They average, over the distribution of $S$, the IPM between the conditional and the marginal distribution, and define EIPM as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)
  :=\int_{\mathcal{S}}
    \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s},\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}})
  \,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_S(s).
  \label{eq:eipm}
\end{equation}
If $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}$ separates probability distributions, then $\operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)=0$ implies $\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=s}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Z}}$ for $\mathbb{P}_S$-almost every $s$.

FREM estimates the conditional distribution at each observed $S_i$ by kernel weighting.  Given the sample $\mathcal{D}=\{(\mathbf{X}_i,S_i,Y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ and $\mathbf{Z}_i=h(\mathbf{X}_i)$, FREM defines
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i),\gamma}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=S_i}
  :=\sum_{j\ne i}\widehat{w}^{(-i)}_{\gamma}(j)\,\delta_{\mathbf{Z}_j},
  \qquad
  \widehat{w}^{(-i)}_{\gamma}(j)
  :=\frac{K_{\gamma}(S_j,S_i)}{\sum_{r\ne i}K_{\gamma}(S_r,S_i)},
  \label{eq:frem-conditional}
\end{equation}
where $K_{\gamma}$ is a kernel on $\mathcal{S}$ with bandwidth $\gamma>0$, $\delta_{\mathbf{z}}$ denotes the point mass at $\mathbf{z}$, and the superscript $(-i)$ indicates that observation $i$ is left out.  FREM compares this kernel-weighted conditional distribution with the leave-one-out empirical marginal distribution $\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i)}_{\mathbf{Z}}=(n-1)^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}\delta_{\mathbf{Z}_j}$, which yields the estimator
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\operatorname{EIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S)
  :=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}
  \left(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i),\gamma}_{\mathbf{Z}\mid S=S_i},
        \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(-i)}_{\mathbf{Z}}\right).
  \label{eq:eipm-estimator}
\end{equation}
With a general discriminator class, this estimator requires a separate maximization at every $S_i$.  FREM avoids these maximizations by taking $\mathcal{V}$ to be the unit ball of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS), for which each IPM is the MMD and has a closed-form kernel expression \citep{gretton2012kernel,kong2025fair}.  For a supervised loss $\ell$ and a penalty parameter $\lambda\ge 0$, FREM then trains the encoder and the prediction head by minimizing
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \ell\!\left(f(\mathbf{Z}_i),Y_i\right)
  +\lambda\,\widehat{\operatorname{EIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}}(\mathbf{Z};S).
  \label{eq:frem-objective}
\end{equation}

\subsection{ReLU-IPM}
\label{sec:relu-ipm}

The RKHS unit ball of Section~\ref{sec:frem} determines the prediction heads that EIPM controls.  The RKHS of the Gaussian kernel, however, contains only infinitely differentiable functions \citep{park2025relu}, so a prediction head of lower smoothness lies outside $\mathcal{V}$.  \citet{park2025relu} address this restriction with ReLU-IPM, an IPM whose discriminator class is indexed by a finite-dimensional parameter and which nevertheless controls every prediction head of bounded H\"older norm.

Let $\mathbb{B}^d:=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^d:\lVert \mathbf{z}\rVert_2\le 1\}$ be the unit ball and $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^d:\lVert\theta\rVert_2=1\}$ the unit sphere.  \citet{park2025relu} define the ReLU discriminator class on $\mathbb{B}^d$ by
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}
  :=\left\{
    v_{\theta,\mu}(\mathbf{z})=(\theta^\top \mathbf{z}+\mu)_+:
    \theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\ \mu\in[-1,1]
  \right\},
  \label{eq:relu-class}
\end{equation}
where $(a)_+:=\max\{a,0\}$.  The ReLU-IPM between probability distributions $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$ is $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})$, that is, Equation~\ref{eq:prelim-ipm} with $\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$.  Although $\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$ is indexed by the finite-dimensional parameter $(\theta,\mu)$ alone, ReLU-IPM separates probability distributions on $\mathbb{B}^d$ \citep{park2025relu}.  The finite dimension of $(\theta,\mu)$ makes this supremum computable: \citet{park2025relu} evaluate ReLU-IPM between empirical distributions by gradient ascent over $(\theta,\mu)$, projecting $\theta$ back onto the unit sphere after each step.

To describe the prediction heads that ReLU-IPM controls, fix a smoothness index $\beta>0$ and let $k$ be the largest integer strictly smaller than $\beta$.  Let $\partial^{\mathbf{a}}$ denote the partial derivative indexed by a multi-index $\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{N}_0^d$ of order $|\mathbf{a}|=a_1+\cdots+a_d$.  Then the $\beta$-H\"older norm of a $k$ times continuously differentiable function $v:\mathbb{B}^d\to\mathbb{R}$ and the $\beta$-H\"older ball on $\mathbb{B}^d$ are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}}
  &:=\max\left\{
    \max_{|\mathbf{a}|\le k}\,\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{B}^d}
      \left|\partial^{\mathbf{a}}v(\mathbf{z})\right|,\;
    \max_{|\mathbf{a}|=k}\,\sup_{\mathbf{z}\ne\mathbf{z}'\in\mathbb{B}^d}
      \frac{\left|\partial^{\mathbf{a}}v(\mathbf{z})-\partial^{\mathbf{a}}v(\mathbf{z}')\right|}
           {\lVert\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{z}'\rVert_2^{\beta-k}}
  \right\},
  \\
  \mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d
  &:=\left\{v\in C^{k}(\mathbb{B}^d):\lVert v\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}}\le 1\right\},
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:holder-ball}
\end{equation}
and H\"older-IPM denotes $\operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}$.  \citet{park2025relu} establish that, for every $\beta>0$ with $\beta\ne(d+3)/2$, there exists a constant $C$ depending only on $d$ and $\beta$ such that, for all probability measures $\mathbb{P}$ and $\mathbb{Q}$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})
  \le C
  \begin{cases}
    \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})^{\frac{2\beta}{d+3}},
      & \beta<\frac{d+3}{2},\\[2mm]
    \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q}),
      & \beta>\frac{d+3}{2}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:relu-holder}
\end{equation}
A bound on ReLU-IPM therefore implies a bound on the H\"older-IPM.

\section{supIPM for fair representation learning}
\label{sec:supipm}

\subsection{From GDP to supIPM}
\label{sec:supipm-criterion}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{motivation.pdf}
  \caption{The conditional and the marginal distribution of the representation coincide at a typical sensitive value and differ at $s^{\star}$, a sensitive value where few observations lie.}
  \label{fig:motivation}
\end{figure}

\citet{jiang2022gdp} define GDP for a continuous sensitive attribute as the average over $S$ of the absolute difference between the conditional and the marginal mean prediction.  For a bounded measurable function $g:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}$,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{GDP}(g)
  :=\mathbb{E}_{S}\!\left[
    \left|
      \mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S]
      -\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})]
    \right|
  \right].
  \label{eq:gdp}
\end{equation}

Because Equation~\ref{eq:gdp} averages this discrepancy over $S$, a large discrepancy confined to a region of small $\mathbb{P}_S$-probability can contribute little to $\operatorname{GDP}(g)$.  Figure~\ref{fig:motivation} illustrates such a case: the discrepancy between the conditional and the marginal distribution is large at the worst-case value $s^{\star}$, whose neighborhood carries too little probability to affect the average over $S$.  Categorical DP, by contrast, imposes one fairness level on every group regardless of its probability.  An extension to a continuous sensitive attribute should meet the same standard at every sensitive value.  We therefore replace the average by a supremum and define $L_{\infty}$-GDP, written $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}$, as
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}(g)
  :=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
  \left|
    \mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s]
    -\mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})]
  \right|.
  \label{eq:linfty-gdp}
\end{equation}
The supremum in Equation~\ref{eq:linfty-gdp} requires the conditional expectation at every $s\in\mathcal{S}$.
% , not only for $\mathbb{P}_S$-almost every $s$.  
We impose the following condition on the distribution of $S$.
\begin{assumption}[Positive density]
\label{as:positive-density}
$S$ has a density $p_S$ with respect to the Lebesgue measure $\mathrm{Leb}$ on $\mathcal{S}$, and $0<c_S\le p_S(s)\le C_S<\infty$ for every $s\in\mathcal{S}$.
\end{assumption}
Assumption~\ref{as:positive-density} is adopted from FREM \citep{kong2025fair} and is maintained throughout.  Let $p(\mathbf{x},s)$ be a joint density of $(\mathbf{X},S)$ with respect to $\nu\otimes\mathrm{Leb}$ for a $\sigma$-finite measure $\nu$ on $\mathcal{X}$.  Fixing a version of $p$ with $\int_{\mathcal{X}} p(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x})=p_S(s)$ for every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, we obtain a version of the conditional expectation that is defined at every $s$, namely
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[g(\mathbf{X})\mid S=s]
  :=\frac{1}{p_S(s)}\int_{\mathcal{X}} g(\mathbf{x})\,p(\mathbf{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\mathbf{x}),
  \qquad s\in\mathcal{S},
  \label{eq:pointwise-conditional}
\end{equation}
and this version is used in what follows, in particular inside the supremum in Equation~\ref{eq:linfty-gdp}.

The value $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}(f\circ h)$ can be evaluated only once the prediction head $f$ is fixed.  We therefore propose a criterion for the representation itself, which controls an entire class of prediction heads at once.  Such a criterion is the IPM of a discriminator class.

Because the discriminator classes of Section~\ref{sec:relu-ipm} are defined on $\mathbb{B}^d$, we map the representation $\mathbf{Z}=h_{\phi}(\mathbf{X})$ of the parameterized encoder $h_{\phi}$ in Section~\ref{sec:frem} into the unit ball by $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}:=\mathbf{Z}/\sqrt{\lVert\mathbf{Z}\rVert_2^2+1}\in\mathbb{B}^d$.  From here on, prediction heads and discriminator classes are defined on $\mathbb{B}^d$ and applied to $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}$.  The conditional distribution $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ is defined as in Equation~\ref{eq:pointwise-conditional}.  For a discriminator class $\mathcal{V}$ on $\mathbb{B}^d$, we define
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}}
  \left(\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s},\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}\right) \notag\\
  &=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
  \left|
    \mathbb{E}\!\left[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\mid S=s\right]
    -\mathbb{E}\!\left[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\right]
  \right|.
  \label{eq:supipm}
\end{align}
Equation~\ref{eq:supipm} is the representation-level counterpart of $\operatorname{GDP}_{\infty}$: it measures the largest discrepancy between the conditional and marginal distributions before a prediction head is chosen.

By Equation~\ref{eq:supipm}, the constraint $\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)\le\rho$ for a fairness level $\rho\ge0$ bounds $\left|\mathbb{E}[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})\mid S=s]-\mathbb{E}[v(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}})]\right|$ by $\rho$ for every discriminator $v\in\mathcal{V}$ and every $s\in\mathcal{S}$.  For comparison with EIPM, write $\operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$ for Equation~\ref{eq:eipm} with $\mathbf{Z}$ replaced by $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}$.  Because an average never exceeds a supremum, $\operatorname{EIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)\le\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$, so a supIPM bound of $\rho$ implies an EIPM bound at the same fairness level.  The converse fails without further regularity in $s$.

It remains to choose the discriminator class $\mathcal{V}$.  Because $\mathcal{V}$ fixes the prediction heads that supIPM controls, a guarantee for every head of bounded H\"older norm calls for the H\"older ball $\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d$.  The H\"older-IPM cannot be computed directly, so we take $\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}$, the ReLU discriminator class of Section~\ref{sec:relu-ipm}, for which the inner IPM in Equation~\ref{eq:supipm} is the ReLU-IPM.  The inequality of \citet{park2025relu} in Equation~\ref{eq:relu-holder} applies to $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ and $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}$ at every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, so a bound on $\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$ implies a bound on $\operatorname{supIPM}_{\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)$.

\subsection{Estimation of supIPM}
\label{sec:supipm-estimation}

For the sample $\mathcal{D}$ of Section~\ref{sec:frem}, let $\mathbf{Z}_i=h_{\phi}(\mathbf{X}_i)$ and $\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i=\mathbf{Z}_i/\sqrt{\lVert\mathbf{Z}_i\rVert_2^2+1}$.  Estimating supIPM requires the conditional distribution $\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ at every $s\in\mathcal{S}$, whereas FREM requires it only at the observed values $S_i$.  We therefore adapt the kernel-weighted construction of Equation~\ref{eq:frem-conditional} to an arbitrary $s$.  Because $s$ is no longer tied to an observation, the leave-one-out correction is not needed, and all $n$ observations are used in both the conditional and the marginal estimates:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)
  :=\frac{K_{\gamma}(S_i,s)}{\sum_{j=1}^n K_{\gamma}(S_j,s)},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}
  :=\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)\,
  \delta_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}
  :=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i},
  \label{eq:supipm-estimators}
\end{equation}
where $K_{\gamma}$ is a kernel on $\mathcal{S}$ with bandwidth $\gamma>0$.  Observations whose sensitive values are close to $s$ receive larger weights.  The weights sum to one over $i$, so that $\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s}$ is a probability distribution.  Substituting the two empirical distributions into Equation~\ref{eq:supipm} yields
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  (\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)
  &:=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
  \operatorname{IPM}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  \left(\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{\gamma}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}\mid S=s},
        \widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}}\right) \notag\\
  &=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}
    \sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\,\mu\in[-1,1]}
  \left|
    \sum_{i=1}^n
    \left(\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)-\frac{1}{n}\right)
    (\theta^\top\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i+\mu)_+
  \right|.
  \label{eq:empirical-supipm}
\end{align}
Whereas the EIPM estimator in Equation~\ref{eq:eipm-estimator} averages the discrepancy at the observed sensitive values, Equation~\ref{eq:empirical-supipm} takes the largest estimated discrepancy over all of $\mathcal{S}$.  When $K_{\gamma}$ is differentiable, the weights are differentiable in $s$, so gradient ascent over $\mathcal{S}$ can search for the largest discrepancy.  
% Section~\ref{sec:supipm-objective} performs this search during training.

\subsection{Learning with the supIPM penalty}
\label{sec:supipm-objective}

We use the empirical supIPM as a penalty and train the encoder $h_{\phi}$ and the prediction head $f_{\psi}$ jointly by minimizing
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\phi,\psi)
  :=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n
  \ell\!\left(f_{\psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i),Y_i\right)
  +\lambda\,
  \widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}
  (\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S),
  \label{eq:supipm-penalized-objective}
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is the supervised loss of Section~\ref{sec:frem} and $\lambda\ge0$ controls the accuracy--fairness trade-off.  Equation~\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective} is the Lagrangian relaxation of minimizing the empirical supervised risk subject to $\widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)\le\rho$ for a fairness level $\rho\ge0$, as in Section~\ref{sec:supipm-criterion}.

Minimizing Equation~\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective} requires evaluating the empirical supIPM in Equation~\ref{eq:empirical-supipm}, which contains two suprema: an outer supremum over the sensitive value $s\in\mathcal{S}$ and an inner supremum over the discriminator parameter $(\theta,\mu)$.  For $s\in\mathcal{S}$ and $(\theta,\mu)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]$, let $\Delta(s;\theta,\mu)$ denote the discrepancy detected at $s$ by the discriminator $v_{\theta,\mu}$:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta(s;\theta,\mu)
  :=\left|
    \sum_{i=1}^n
    \left(\widehat{w}_{\gamma}(i;s)-\frac{1}{n}\right)
    (\theta^\top\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i+\mu)_+
  \right|.
  \label{eq:discriminator-discrepancy}
\end{equation}
Then $\widehat{\operatorname{supIPM}}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}};S)=\sup_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\sup_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\,\mu\in[-1,1]}\Delta(s;\theta,\mu)$, so the minimization of Equation~\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective} is a min--max problem, which we solve by alternating gradient descent in $(\phi,\psi)$ with projected gradient ascent in $s$ and in $(\theta,\mu)$.

Because gradient ascent can converge to a local maximum, we follow \citet{park2025relu} and perform the ascent from several initial values in parallel, maintaining $T$ candidate sensitive values $\mathbf{s}:=(s^{(1)},\ldots,s^{(T)})$ and $M$ discriminators $\mathbf{v}:=((\theta_1,\mu_1),\ldots,(\theta_M,\mu_M))$, where $(\theta_m,\mu_m)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]$ for $m\in[M]:=\{1,\ldots,M\}$.  Let $\hat{s}:=\argmax_{s\in\{s^{(1)},\ldots,s^{(T)}\}}\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s;\theta_m,\mu_m)$ be the candidate at which the largest discrepancy is detected.  The candidates and the discriminators are updated by gradient ascent on
\begin{equation}
  G(\mathbf{s}):=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s^{(t)};\theta_m,\mu_m),
  \qquad
  H(\mathbf{v}):=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Delta(\hat{s};\theta_m,\mu_m),
  \label{eq:stacked-objectives}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}$ and $\hat{s}$ are held fixed at their current values in $G$ and in $H$, respectively.  The $t$-th summand of $G$ depends on $\mathbf{s}$ only through $s^{(t)}$, and the $m$-th summand of $H$ depends on $\mathbf{v}$ only through $(\theta_m,\mu_m)$, so one ascent step on $G$ moves all $T$ candidates and one ascent step on $H$ moves all $M$ discriminators.  We write $\Pi_{A}$ for the projection onto a set $A$.  After each ascent step, $\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}$ returns each candidate to the sensitive domain, and $\Pi_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]}$ returns each $\theta_m$ to the unit sphere and each $\mu_m$ to $[-1,1]$.  The encoder and the prediction head are then updated by gradient descent on $\mathcal{L}(\phi,\psi)$ with the empirical supIPM replaced by $\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(\hat{s};\theta_m,\mu_m)$, that is, with the outer supremum taken over the $T$ candidates and the inner supremum over the $M$ discriminators.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:supipm-frl} assembles these three updates into $R$ iterations, and we name the resulting procedure supIPM-FRL.  Each iteration performs $J_v$ discriminator updates, each preceded by $J_s$ ascent steps of freshly initialized candidates, and closes with one descent step.  The discriminator updates, the candidate ascent steps and the descent step use the step sizes $\eta_v$, $\eta_s$ and $\eta$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{supIPM-FRL}
\label{alg:supipm-frl}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE sample $\mathcal{D}$, supervised loss $\ell$, kernel $K_{\gamma}$ with bandwidth $\gamma$, penalty parameter $\lambda$, number of candidates $T$, number of discriminators $M$
\ENSURE encoder $h_{\phi}$ and prediction head $f_{\psi}$
\STATE Initialize $\phi$, $\psi$, and $\mathbf{v}$ with $(\theta_m,\mu_m)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]$ for $m\in[M]$
\FOR{$r=1,\ldots,R$}
  \FOR{$j=1,\ldots,J_v$}
    \STATE Initialize $\mathbf{s}$ at $T$ distinct points of $\mathcal{S}$ \label{alg:s-init}
    \FOR{$J_s$ steps}
      \STATE $\mathbf{s}\leftarrow\Pi_{\mathcal{S}}\!\left(\mathbf{s}+\eta_s\,\nabla_{\mathbf{s}}G(\mathbf{s})\right)$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE $\hat{s}\leftarrow\argmax_{s\in\{s^{(1)},\ldots,s^{(T)}\}}\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(s;\theta_m,\mu_m)$ \label{alg:s-star}
    \STATE $\mathbf{v}\leftarrow\Pi_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-1,1]}\!\left(\mathbf{v}+\eta_v\,\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}H(\mathbf{v})\right)$
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE Recompute $\hat{s}$ by repeating lines~\ref{alg:s-init}--\ref{alg:s-star} with the updated $\mathbf{v}$
  \STATE $(\phi,\psi)\leftarrow(\phi,\psi)-\eta\,\nabla_{(\phi,\psi)}\big[\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\ell(f_{\psi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_i),Y_i)+\lambda\max_{m\in[M]}\Delta(\hat{s};\theta_m,\mu_m)\big]$ \label{alg:encoder-step}
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $h_{\phi}$, $f_{\psi}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Equations~\ref{eq:supipm-penalized-objective} and \ref{eq:discriminator-discrepancy} are built from sample averages, so the procedure applies unchanged when these averages are computed on the current minibatch.

The text must be confined within a rectangle 5.5~inches (33~picas) wide and

preferred typeface throughout. Paragraphs are separated by 1/2~line space,
with no indentation.

Paper title is 17~point, in small caps and left-aligned.
All pages should start at 1~inch (6~picas) from the top of the page.

Authors' names are
set in boldface, and each name is placed above its corresponding
address. The lead author's name is to be listed first, and

regarding figures, tables, acknowledgments, and references.
